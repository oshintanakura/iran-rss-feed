💧 Environmental water rights: from allocation on paper to water reaching the wetland

The report "Regulatory Oversight of Environmental Water Rights Allocation under Table (7) of Article (37) of the Seventh Development Plan Law," prepared by Mehdi Rajabi and Ali Haji-Moradi in Shahrivar 1405, examines the gap between legal commitments and the actual delivery of water needed by ecosystems.

The report's findings for the water year 1403–1404 show that drought is not the only reason water rights are not being fulfilled; weaknesses in the allocation system, withdrawals along the route, lack of coordination among agencies, and the absence of transparent monitoring also play a role.

The report's central message is clear: releasing water from a dam alone does not mean environmental water rights have been secured. The evaluation criterion must be whether water reaches the target ecosystem, in a volume, quality, and timing suited to its needs.

The report emphasizes reforming water allocation under water-scarce conditions, online monitoring and public disclosure of data, controlling withdrawals along the route, and integrated management of river basins.

🌱 Environmental water rights must hold a stable place in water allocation decisions — a share whose provision does not depend on water left over after other uses.
