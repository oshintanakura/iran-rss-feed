Superadobe is an expanded method of the same old adobe, using long bags filled with moistened soil and then tamped down for proper compaction. The bags are stacked row by row in a circular plan, rising uniformly to form a dome. To prevent the bags from shifting and also to resist earthquakes, barbed wire is used between the layers, acting like mortar to bind the rows together and helping give the structure an integrated, chain-like quality that gives it high resistance to earthquakes and other events. In this structure, due to the dome form, the thickness of the walls, and the natural materials used, heat exchange with the outside is minimized and energy loss is managed dramatically. Superadobe was invented by Nader Khalili, an Iranian architect, and is now recognized worldwide as one of the methods for building sustainable and natural homes.
