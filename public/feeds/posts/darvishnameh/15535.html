♦️ Trees that become victims of war! ♦️📚@darvishnameh✍️ Mangroves—Avicennia, Rhizophora, and in short, all mangrove species—absorb carbon and produce oxygen several times more than any forest habitat we know, and in addition, they are highly effective in bioremediation of water and spawning grounds for aquatic life. But these days, the Qeshm mangrove habitat has become a victim of war, and its shores are covered in tar! We might think this tragedy ends with a few blackened trees! That's not the case at all... That's not what this is about. What has happened is much bigger than a few trees burning and turning black. A mangrove is not just a tree. It's an ecosystem; a living network that much of the coast's and sea's life depends on. When oil enters this ecosystem, it doesn't just stick to the tree trunks; it enters a complete chain of life. And perhaps to understand the scale of this disaster, no explanation is as sufficient as seeing these images. This isn't just oil pollution; part of an ecosystem is being buried under oil.
