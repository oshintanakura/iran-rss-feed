🚨ExxonMobil has spent money on the local community, provided equipment to emergency responders, established scholarships, and its employees have put in thousands of hours of volunteer work. On paper, nearly every element of a corporate social responsibility program is there. And yet, some of the people who are supposed to live alongside one of the company's largest carbon storage projects in Texas are protesting, saying their concerns about water, safety, and the future of their community have not been heard enough.
🚨What makes the story of ExxonMobil's Rose project so intriguing is precisely this contradiction: a company that spends money on its host community, while that same community has questions and objections about its project.
🚨Over the course of 13 years, roughly 53 million tons of carbon dioxide are set to be injected deep underground in southeastern Texas. Exxon says the Rose CCS project is part of the solution for cutting emissions from America's heavy industries. But local residents have a simpler question: if the carbon from big industries is going to be buried beneath our land, who can guarantee our water, our safety, and the future of this region?
🚨The Texas Railroad Commission, the state body that regulates oil, gas, and underground storage, issued the project permit in September by a two-to-one vote. The key detail was that the dissenting vote came not from an environmental group, but from one of the commission's own three members.
