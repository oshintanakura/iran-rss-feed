The play "Cyrus" is not a return to the past for me; it is looking at today through the mirror of history. In writing this play, rather than seeking to construct a mythical figure of Cyrus, I have thought about a human being who, amid power, violence, and fear, faces a difficult choice: the sword or the word. History, for me, is not a mass of names and conquests; it is the memory of the people who lived in it; people who were afraid, who loved, who betrayed, who stood firm, and who made choices at fateful moments. "Cyrus" is an attempt to hear the silenced voices of these very people; to see the human beings who have sometimes been forgotten behind the great names of history. As a director, too, I have not wanted to reconstruct history; I have wanted to summon it into the present; so that perhaps we may realize that the distance between that era and our era is not as far as we think. "Cyrus" is not the narrative of a conqueror; it is the search for a human being who wants to take power beyond the mere meaning of domination. Perhaps that is why, for me, "Cyrus" is more about the possibility of remaining human in the midst of power than about the past.#نادر_برهانی_مرند@moaser_theater
