Two Separate Roles
Our Prophet Muhammad, peace be upon him, played two great roles in history.
His first role was prophetic. He presented his profound spiritual experience of the oneness of God, the universe, the afterlife, and the path to human happiness as a worldview in the form of verses focused on good conduct and individual and collective morality, to serve as a guide for seekers forever.
His second role was the political leadership of a small community, entrusted to him through an agreement by the people of Medina, which was distinct in nature from his prophetic role. Muhammad spent thirteen years in his birthplace, Mecca, as only a prophet, enduring much harassment and persecution from his people.
The last ten years of our Prophet's life, in addition to his prophetic mission, involved the political leadership of the people of Yathrib, which he renamed Medina—meaning the city or a center of civility and lawfulness as opposed to savagery and lawlessness.
The Prophet was a highly skilled, realistic, and capable political leader. As the demands of politics required, he made treaties, fought, made peace, forgave, took a hard line, retreated, and advanced.
The prophetic mission addresses humanity "as it ought to be," while political leadership manages humanity "as it is."
Hence, Muhammad's prophetic messages are a transhistorical and eternal legacy, while his political leadership is subject to the changing and evolving nature of the times, carrying all the complexities, tragedies, and necessary flexibilities of relative matters.
Our Prophet did not see political affairs as a pre-discovered and predetermined truth; he put them to consultation and made decisions through deliberation with the people of Medina. Some believers and non-believers in Muhammad's faith lack the ability to distinguish between these two roles, and thus misunderstand his great undertaking.
