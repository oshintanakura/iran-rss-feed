Critical ConversationsMohammad FazeliFor a society that has experienced the bloody and catastrophic events of Aban 98 (November 2019), dialogue about any plan related to changing gasoline and energy policy is vital. For a society where the energy imbalance has reached a life-or-death threshold, dialogue about any plan related to changing gasoline and energy policy is vital. The conversation with Dr. Mohsen Yazdanpanah, an economist, published on the Iran Talk media outlet under the title "Gasoline Reforms: Without Fireworks," was an attempt to open a dialogue about the plan some in the government are pursuing, critique it, and show its consequences.Watching "Gasoline Reforms: Without Fireworks" — any plan can be critiqued, including the current government's plan; and any critique can also be critiqued, including the critiques Mohsen Yazdanpanah levels at the current plan. What matters is that for any chronic and complex issue—such as energy in today's Iran—there is no better path than policy transparency, social dialogue, and building social and political consensus on the best solutions. All those interested in energy policy and debates on price and non-price reforms in gasoline and other energy carriers can be invited to watch this conversation and join the social debate about it. These are critical conversations.
