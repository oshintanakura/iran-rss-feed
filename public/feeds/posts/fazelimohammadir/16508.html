America from Within: The Sociology of the Iran-US Conflict
Dr. Mahmoud Sadri, an Iranian sociologist living in the United States with fifty years of experience living, researching, and teaching in American society and universities, discusses the inner characteristics of American society, the role of political currents, extremist religious movements, and social forces in shaping US policy toward Iran. Watch the interview: https://youtu.be/G5WkWgwBKIM

Mahmoud Sadri believes there is no irreconcilable structural conflict of interests in Iran-US relations and that with more reasonable behavior and a bit of historical luck, relations between Iran and the US can be improved. He argues that actions known as extremism serve no benefit to Iran's national interests and only strengthen the discourse of anti-Iranian extremists in America. He also believes Iran suffers greatly from a lack of proper public diplomacy and absence of voice in American society and politics, to the extent that Iran-hatred is cost-free for everyone in the US.
