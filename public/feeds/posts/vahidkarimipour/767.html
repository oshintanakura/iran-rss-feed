A word to students

After the letter from 46 professors of Sharif University of Technology titled "For Sharif" was sent—which was in fact these colleagues' response to the "Letter from the Mothers of Students Expelled from Sharif"—I and at least four other colleagues who, unfortunately, did not have the honor of writing that letter but had signed it, received numerous messages from some students. All of them, while maintaining a polite tone, asked: "What was your feeling as signatories of this letter when the attack on Minab school happened?" and "Why did these professors not issue a statement at that time and condemn it?" This text is a response to those students.

The attack on Minab school and the killing of innocent children is undoubtedly a war crime. I hope that after the war ends, it will be pursued through an official complaint filed by the government and that appropriate compensation will be granted to the victims' families, although receiving compensation will never bring those innocent children back to their families' arms nor cool that eternal grief.

Therefore, the right way to deal with such atrocities is to pursue them through governmental and international institutions, not to have them condemned by this or that group of citizens. If the professors' letter had merely condemned the expulsion of students, it would have been a useless act as well. Instead, this letter contains a specific demand from the university president to review these rulings in accordance with revised procedures.

And now a word to the students who are dissatisfied with this letter and still support the expulsion of a group of their classmates. In my opinion, in any country in the world, the human and moral duty of a citizen is to side with a fellow citizen when they are up against organizations and institutions, to listen to their words, and to demand fair treatment for them, rather than siding with that organization or institution. The reason is very simple: because even an organization like the municipality is powerful enough and, when facing a citizen, does not need the support of another citizen. Even if a student lives in the most democratic country in the world, when it comes to a student versus an organization, ethically one should stand by one's classmate, not by that organization. For a student, being on a committee where another student—their classmate—receives an expulsion ruling is never a commendable act, and perhaps years later, when the fervor of youth subsides, it will bring shame and regret.
