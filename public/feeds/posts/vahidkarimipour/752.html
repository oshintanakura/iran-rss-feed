On Monday morning, our university's technology center was bombed and completely destroyed by Israel. No one hoped to be able to hold their virtual class session again, and we all thought our education would be closed until the end of this semester, and all students would lose an academic semester, especially when for each student losing a semester means falling behind a future they had planned and worked for years. But immediately, the company operating Skyroom in Iran made its service free for the university, and those involved in the university (the vice-chancellor and educational management and technology engineers) worked until the last hours of the night to create virtual classrooms for each professor, sent the addresses to all professors, informed thousands of students of the new addresses, sent new usernames and passwords in prepared lists to everyone, and presumably dozens of other tasks hidden from non-specialists. Virtual classes started again, and instead of a semester, only one day was interrupted. This was not a simple event, but a great and proud achievement that shows Iran is not just the Islamic Republic government, but a very worthy, respectable, and honorable ecosystem born of the creativity, knowledge, and efficiency of a wide range of specialists and patriots with various political thoughts and beliefs. Such an ecosystem must be defended with all our might against foreigners, ill-wishers, and the naive. Years ago, a European politician asked a mathematician, "Do these abstract things you do even help defend the nation?" and received the answer, "No, they don't help defend, but they make our nation worthy of defense." Our nation, too, is worthy of defense because of its abundance of intellectual, artistic, and industrial assets. Let us hope that the government also appreciates this ecosystem and compensates for its past mistakes so that all of us together—both those who stayed in Iran and that vast host of writers, artists, literati, and scientists who were forced to leave their homeland but have their hearts tied to their birthplace—can strive for the development of a prosperous and free Iran.
