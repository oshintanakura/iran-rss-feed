The following letter has been written by a group of mothers of students at Sharif University of Technology to one of our colleagues, and in fact to the entire body of colleagues at Sharif University of Technology. Its audience is, in reality, all professors and officials of Iranian universities.——————————————————————————————————————————————————————————————————-We are a group of mothers whose children have, in recent months, faced heavy disciplinary rulings and expulsion from university. We write this letter not out of confrontation, nor to disregard the rules, but from the position of mothers who are worried about the future, the fate, and the lost years of youth of their children, and we have turned to you with great hope. Our children may have made mistakes or committed violations under certain circumstances, and it is natural that the university has the right to react to violations; but is taking away the opportunity to continue education and destroying the future path of a young person the only possible response to a mistake? What pains us most of all is the severity and sometimes irreparability of the punishment that affects the future of these young people. Many of these students have strived for years to get into Sharif University, have completed a significant portion of their studies, and their families have placed all their hope and resources into their future. What is most painful for us is that some of these students, in addition to the disciplinary case at the university and the expulsion ruling, have also faced punishment from the judicial system (mostly one to three years of imprisonment); that is, for a single offense at the university, they have faced two punishments from two institutions—the university and the judicial system! We ask you, if possible, to hear the voice of these mothers and, to the extent of your ability, help in reviewing the expulsion rulings, converting heavy punishments into more proportionate rulings, and providing the possibility for these students to return to the path of education. Sometimes a second chance can save a young person's life. We do not expect you to disregard the law; we only ask that alongside the law, fairness, proportionality of punishment, the circumstances of each student, and the future of a human being also be considered. If you have the means to follow up on these concerns or convey them to university officials or officials of the Ministry of Science, we sincerely request that you do not leave us alone. We thank you in advance for the time you take to hear our voice. With respect and hope, a group of mothers of expelled students of Sharif University of Technology
