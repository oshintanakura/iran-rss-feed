Thanks to a dear friend living in Europe, I recently received images of the paper copies of my three recent books, "In the Struggle of Suffering and Meaning," "In the Passage of Time: A Stroll in the Garden of Bu Sa'id," and "Velvet Whispers: Existential Murmurs," which have been published over the past three years. Looking at these images and then reviewing the table of contents of the aforementioned trilogy, the many hours, days, and weeks spent on contemplation, research, and writing them paraded before my eyes and blew a pleasant feeling toward me. Among the essays included in these books, I personally prefer "Dostoevsky; the Modern Pilgrim of Thoughtful Suffering," "The Modern Pilgrim, Touching Presence, and Experiencing Boredom," and "From Correspondence Truth to Persuasive-Narrative Truth"; which not only contain the author's fresh ideas but also, in the process of writing and finalizing them, I savored "flow" as narrated by Csikszentmihalyi to the extent of my ability. A flow that is a blend of challenge, knowledge, and skill and requires spending a great deal of time; a flow that is of the kind of mental and psychological sweating... On the other hand, I deeply love the book "Velvet Whispers," which is of the essence of Soroush Dabbagh and is deeply intertwined with his existence and existential concerns, depicting his very self... These days, alongside my work engagements and commitments, I am writing the introduction to the book "Narratives of Suffering and War." Thanks to the efforts of the editor and the esteemed publisher, the book is on the verge of being finalized.
