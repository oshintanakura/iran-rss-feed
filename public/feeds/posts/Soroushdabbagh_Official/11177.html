Execution, suicide, and the tormented psyche of society; the costs of securitized governance
Soroush Dabbagh - Yesterday, hearing three consecutive pieces of news, I became deeply bitter, and for moments the sky of my eyes turned rainy and tears overwhelmed me. Aida Heidari, the fiancée of Abolfazl Soleimani, one of those who lost their lives in the January protests, ended her life a few days ago. According to the families, Aida and Abolfazl were supposed to marry this very month. Also, Danial Karimi, the father of Amir Mohammad Karimi, one of those who lost their lives in the January protests, ended his life a few days ago. Amir Mohammad Karimi was an athlete and former member of Iran's national youth taekwondo team. In addition, Erfan Mirzaei, a twenty-one-year-old young rapper and singer, and one of those detained during the January protests last year, was executed in Isfahan a few days ago.
Over the past fifteen months, our beloved country has been embroiled in three devastating wars, numerous infrastructures have been destroyed, and several thousand innocent compatriots have been bathed in their own blood. For some time now, a naval siege has been added to the sanctions, placing additional economic pressure on the shoulders of citizens, to the extent that managing daily expenses for millions of Iranians has, according to official statistics published within the country, clearly become an exhausting and distressing ordeal. Moreover, in the January protests last year, several thousand protesting, desperate, and helpless compatriots fell to the ground, bathed in blood, and surrendered their souls to the Creator of life.
Truly, these are strange times. These days, I think a great deal about the dusty and ambiguous tomorrow of our society and country; a stricken society that has endured numerous individual and collective traumas over these same fifteen months...
