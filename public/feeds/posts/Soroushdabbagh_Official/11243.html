13. Sohrab Sepehri was murmuring to himself somewhere in the "Traveler" notebook: "The journey carried me to equatorial lands / and beneath the shade of that tall green 'Banyan' / how well I remember / the phrase that entered the summer pasture of the mind: / Be vast, and alone, and humble, and hard." Sohrab, in his long-extended inner journey—which is of the nature of "horizontal wayfaring," setting out from oneself and arriving at oneself—draws back the curtain on his lived experience and pure illuminations; an experience that occurred beneath a towering tree, caught him unawares, drew him in, and like the Buddha enlightened him and granted him illumination. An experience that blew toward him and suffused every limb of his body and soul, marked a "rebirth" for him, and added to the volume of his "dove-like experiences"... Having breadth of spirit and a capacious digestive capacity makes you and me people of tolerance and civility, and noticeably increases our measure of "tolerance," "respecting the human being," and "accepting the other." Likewise, embracing "existential loneliness"—which is intertwined with your and my being—and reducing its volume with the aid of "velvety solitude," migrates lightness and joy toward us. "Humility and hardness" arrive together with high "self-esteem" and "self-worth" and are among the components of adequate mental health. "Humility" arrives with "realistic modesty," not "affected modesty." The modern wayfarer neither knows nor presents himself as lower than he is, nor considers and displays himself as higher than he is. He loves himself honestly and realistically, as he is, and with an eye to his endowments, capabilities, successes, weaknesses, misfortunes, and failures, deeply; and does not spend night and day seeking others' approval; rather, in Shamlou's phrase, he dwells at the "glorious altitude of humility" and fills and empties his lungs with its pure air. However, in "affected modesty," which is intertwined with low self-esteem, the individual does not believe in what he expresses about himself and in how he behaves, and with the affectation and artificiality that drips from his speech and behavior, he cunningly seeks to obtain others' approval. To the extent that you and I are "vast and alone and humble and hard," we are in a good state and experience mature dealings and relationships with the humans around us; for our existential goblet is filled, and following the Rendi of the Stoic-thinking Shirazi, we flow and drink and give drink.
