"This caravan of life passes wondrously." Twenty years ago, after completing my philosophical studies at the University of Warwick in England, I returned to Tehran and, as a faculty member and assistant professor, began teaching and researching at the "Iranian Institute of Philosophy." During that five-year period, my office was near Dr. Behzad Salki's office. For this reason, on average, we saw each other twice a week; either he would come to my office, or I would go to his, and we would drink tea and talk together. I remember that in our first meetings, we discussed the book "Philosophy of Religion" by John Hick, which had been translated by him and published under the name Behzad Rad. In the 1990s (1370s), when I was a pharmacy student, a few friends interested in philosophical topics and I would read different chapters of Hick's translated book weekly and discuss them. Later, in the 2000s (1380s), we discussed John Hick's "The Fifth Dimension," which had been translated into Persian by Behzad Salki, with several philosophy-researching friends. At that time, I did not know that the translator of both books was the same person, and that Behzad Rad was the pen name of Mr. Behzad Salki. When I mentioned this to Behzad Salki, he laughed and said: "I preferred that at that time, Hick's works, which had just come before the eyes of Persian-speaking philosophy researchers, not be published under my name, because 'the passage of well-being was narrow.'" Dr. Salki was a pleasant, humble, and sincere person, and a tranquility emanated from him. He had nothing to do with anger, jealousy, or harshness, and he enjoyed great mental health. I do not recall him speaking ill of anyone. In our conversations, we talked about the books we were reading and our work and research projects. He was aware of my great fondness for Sepehri and Wittgenstein, and out of kindness, he introduced me to some good Persian and English works in these areas, most of which I read with enthusiasm. He had a close friendship with the esteemed professor Baha' al-Din Khorramshahi.
