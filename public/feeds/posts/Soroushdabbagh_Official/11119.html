Whisper 9. "The solution to the problem of life can be seen in the disappearance of this problem. Is this not the reason why those who, after a long period of doubt, have come to see the meaning of life, have been unable to say what this meaning is? Indeed, there are things that cannot be put into words. They show themselves. They are the mystical." The above passages belong to the groundbreaking work "Tractatus Logico-Philosophicus"; a time when Wittgenstein was fighting in the Austrian army and simultaneously working on the final version of the book, carrying it in his backpack. Undoubtedly, facing death on the front lines had a profound impact on the writing of the final passages of the "Tractatus Logico-Philosophicus," passages that carry strong existential undertones and are deeply intertwined with the being of the young Ludwig. There is no escape from addressing the "meaning of life," for man is a meaning-seeking and meaning-finding animal. A pure insight ripples through Wittgenstein's account of the meaning of life; a fluidity and elusiveness can be seen in it; one can neither ignore it, forget it, nor abandon it, nor can one formulate it neatly and do it justice in language, with the help of words, propositions, and phrases. As Rumi whispered to himself in the "Divan-e Shams": "Catch the hem of His grace, for He may suddenly flee / But do not pull Him like an arrow, for He may escape from the bow." Wittgenstein's tone and manner of phrasing in the above passages evoke the tone and phrases of the "Holy Bible." It is as if, as Jung and Kazantzakis have noted, the young Ludwig is the second creator of the world, seeking to complete the half-finished work of creation. In fact, he has given meaning to his life by producing original creative works and depositing an elixir within them; just as Forough and Sohrab did by creating the poems "Earthly Verses" and "Surah of Watching" in the collections "Another Birth" and "The Green Volume."
