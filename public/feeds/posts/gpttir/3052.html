🔰 The National Artificial Intelligence Development Law: Iran's Entry Point into Regulation or the Trap of Premature Rules

👤 Amirhossein Zanbaq, a researcher in the Regulation Group at the Sharif Governance Think Tank, has written in a note:

🔹The experience of the European AI Act shows that strict regulation, even with high institutional capacity, has run into delays and implementation difficulties. Iran cannot copy the European model, because the main issue is still the formation of the industry, not the control of a mature industry.

🔸The following three key proposals can serve as a starting point for regulation:

▫️The principle should be to facilitate development and regulate in proportion to risk, not to require prior licensing for all applications. Low-risk applications should be governed through ex-post oversight and general rules, and only high-risk applications should be subject to greater oversight.

▫️The National Artificial Intelligence Organization should play the role of coordinator, not serve as the detailed regulator of all sectors. Common national principles should be established, and the details should be left to the regulators of each sector.

▫️Regulation should be accompanied by market development tools.
