Pezeshkian's speech is a narrative of understanding suffering and empathy, not weakness, flaw, or deficiency. His narrative openly articulates problems that have not been a concern for the country's officials for years—not that they deny them, no, they simply were not their concern. Pezeshkian stands with ordinary people and speaks in the language of ordinary people about their daily worries and sufferings. This very understanding of suffering is itself valuable. Saadi, in the Golestan, in the chapter on the conduct of kings, quotes a dervish who lay asleep outside in the cold, saying: 'O you who are secure in the world through your fortune, even if you have no sorrow, do you not have a share in our sorrow?'
