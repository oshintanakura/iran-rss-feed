◀️ Daily Note⭕️ From "The Tenants" to "A House on the Verge of Collapse"; Iranian Society Through the Lens of Decline🔻 Hadi Soleimani Qarah Gol, PhD in Sociology, Kharazmi University🔍The note "From The Tenants to A House on the Verge of Collapse" by Hadi Soleimani Qarah Gol, by comparing two cinematic images of "home," critiques narratives that view Iranian society primarily through the lens of crisis, loss, and decline.📚The author, referring to Mohsen Qaraei's film "The Caretaker," with the international title Falling House, and Dariush Mehrjui's film "The Tenants," shows that the home in Iranian cinema has repeatedly become a metaphor for narrating the collective condition; from a house caught in strife and decay to a house on the verge of demolition. These two films do not carry the same meaning, but both provide the possibility of speaking about society through the condition of the house.In continuation, the note considers this metaphor part of a long-standing pattern in thinking about Iran; a pattern that narrates society through the lens of backwardness, disorder, rupture, and collapse. The author emphasizes that real crises such as migration, distrust, poverty, generational conflict, and weak institutions do not in themselves mean the collapse of society, and "many problems" should not be equated with "decline."💬Against this narrative, the author speaks of "defending society"; a defense that means neither denying crises nor defending the status quo, but rather an effort to see society beyond its list of afflictions.
