⚫️ Given my interest in the sociology of religious authority and the years of research I've done in this area, friends repeatedly put this question to me: which marja' taqlid in Qom do you personally prefer most? Each time, my answer was Ayatollah al-Uzma Shobeiry Zanjani. A first-rate jurist, and outstanding in rijal. But he combined this jurisprudential and scholarly standing with qualities that are rarer still. He, his household, and those around him lived with great integrity and an intense sensitivity to financial propriety. He was humble in a way seldom seen and indifferent to the conventional protocols of marja'iyat, and despite absolute jurisprudential authority, he made no effort whatsoever to attract devotees and followers or to acquire prestige, status, fame, or power. He was spiritual—a "man of God"—and his serenity, his piety, and his prayers were a sight to behold. He was one of those of whom it is said, in a saying attributed to Jesus Christ: "Whoever's sight reminds you of God, whose knowledge adds to your knowledge, and whose deeds make you desire goodness." In addition to his morning teaching, he held a gathering at his home an hour before maghrib, which in my view—if the hawza's "scholarly gathering" were ever to be registered as an "intangible heritage"—would be a perfect example; and for me, a mere outsider, every time I attended, its atmosphere and details were a spectacle to behold. He also possessed a cultivated aesthetic sensibility, evident in, among other things, his beautiful handwriting and his exceedingly delicate gaze and body language. He never sought perks, resources, or privileges, and he used his considerable spiritual influence over the system's high-ranking officials solely to unblock people's difficulties and help the oppressed. A lovable spiritual jurist. May his soul rest in peace.https://t.me/ShobeiryZanjani/6357@jalaeipour
