I too want an "honorable peace." I truly do. And when I speak of an "honorable peace," I don't mean a political slogan. For me, it's very simple: I want a peace in which the people of Iran are not forced to accept every threat and imposition just to survive. An honorable peace means a peace in which a nation is not constantly dragged to the negotiating table under the threat of civilizational destruction, assassination, attacks on infrastructure, and the destruction of its power plants and bridges. A peace that does not mean "either accept our terms, or it's time for the next assassination or attack." "Mowing the lawn" and assassination cannot be the strategy of a lasting peace. A peace built on fear and threat is more like a temporary ceasefire between two rounds of violence. I want peace, but a peace in which Iran can live with security and dignity—not a peace in which one side holds a gun over the other's head and asks it to call that situation "peace." To achieve such a peace, there is no way but deterrence. Deterrence does not mean warmongering; its logic is precisely the opposite: that the cost of war is so clear that no side is tempted to start it. Real peace becomes possible when the other side knows that Iran cannot be forced into submission through threats of assassination, attack, and the destruction of its infrastructure. An honorable peace is a peace without war; but to remain without war, one must also have the capability to prevent war.
