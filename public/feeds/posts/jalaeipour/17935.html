The book traces the trajectory of his life, his positions, and his intellectual evolution over the past five decades. In it, Fukuyama explains how, under the influence of political and economic developments, he moved beyond the theory of the "End of History" and no longer sees the future of liberal democracy as guaranteed. Once known as one of the ideologues of the American right, he now considers himself a social democrat and stresses the need for wealth redistribution and stronger labor unions. He says he held two deep beliefs, both of which have been undermined by the developments of recent years: the belief that American military intervention can help spread democracy, and the belief in a maximally free market. The results of the American military attack on Iraq shattered the first belief, and the 2008 financial crisis shattered the second. He sees the rise of Trump in America as a sign of democracy's fragile state in the free market, and stresses that societies must have credible institutions to regulate the market and preserve democracy's institutional capacities.
