While the world faces an intensifying climate crisis, deepening inequality, erosion of social trust, and extreme concentration of wealth, governments continue to seek to patch up markets, and economics has also viewed the "common good"—whether in the sense of public goods or common resources—only from the perspective of correcting market or government failures.
Mazzucato, professor at University College London and founder of the Institute for Innovation and Public Purpose, in her new book titled "The Common Good Economy: A New Compass" addresses the need to rethink the relationship between government, market, and citizens. In her view, the government should go beyond the role of repairing market failures and actively steer markets around the common good. She argues that a healthy economy is not built solely by relying on the market, but requires purposeful direction by public institutions. Instead of a minimal view of government, she speaks of a role in which the government coordinates businesses and society around shared missions—missions such as reducing inequality and combating climate change.
