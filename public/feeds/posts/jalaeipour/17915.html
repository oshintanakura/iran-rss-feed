Today's Iranian media have headlined this sentence from the President's speech at the annual summit of the "West Asia Union": This year, for the first time in West Asia, extreme poverty was eradicated, and we have no hungry, homeless, out-of-school, or treatment-deprived people. I remember all the years we longed to read such a headline. I remember all the elections in which we pushed to make the eradication of extreme poverty a slogan and a priority for the candidates, and twice it happened, but sanctions, war, and misgovernance prevented its full realization. The deep smile of satisfaction that reading this headline has brought to my lips—I cannot hide it on the metro. The unknown old man across from me, wearing a beautiful Kurdish outfit, smiles too upon seeing my smile. We are immersed in the sweetness of our smiles when my daughter calls from Lark, and her image appears on my smart glasses. Simultaneously with the West Asia Union summit, the Hormuz Festival has been held on the islands of Hormuz, Hengam, and Lark. For several years now, it has brought together the best musicians and the most beautiful clothing and artistic rituals of the residents around the Persian Gulf and thousands of participants from across Iran and the Persian Gulf and other countries, and it has become the most popular art festival of the Global South. The presence of any smart robot at the festival is not permitted, and this very rule has made the ecomuseums of these islands more attractive to young people. My daughter, along with two of her classmates, has gone to participate in the festival by electric high-speed train to Bandar Abbas and from there by electric ship to Lark. She is excited about the beauty of the beaches and the art of the participants and the joy of socializing with friends she discovered at this same festival last year. In a three-dimensional video call from Lark, as last year, she insists that we not stay in Tehran and migrate to Hengam Island. Enthusiastically, she recounts that at iftar in the Shahid Tangsiri Mosque of Lark, shrimp do-piazeh and halva were hosted by the locals. She says: "Dad, just let go of Tehran already! Isn't it a shame to miss even a single day of walking along the shore to watch the sunrise or sunset or the moon?" As it happens, her mother and I have had this same wish for years, and since the beginning of our acquaintance and love, our most beautiful memories and experiences have been tied to the islands around Qeshm and the coasts of Hormozgan, but since the time of Corona and then the wars of 1404 and 1405 and the eventful years after, we were forced to stay and help in Tehran. Now that we see our Iran has flourished and for several years has remained the connecting ring of the world, green and safe, and Samaneh's design and my party work and attendance in the girl's classes at Shahid Minab High School can all be followed in cyberspace, perhaps the time has come to act on her suggestion.
