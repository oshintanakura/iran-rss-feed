🎒 Economic sanctions and the educational future of children

There was a time when economic sanctions were presented as an alternative to war, and many hoped this approach could resolve international disputes without war and destruction, while others dismissed it as ineffective. But as time passed, these assumptions were met with fundamental doubts. It gradually became clear that economic sanctions leave irreversible damage in people's lives.

Mohammad Javad Dashti-Manesh, an economic researcher, examines in this piece one of the studies conducted on the intergenerational effects of sanctions on the educational future of Iranian children, emphasizing that economic pressures must not be allowed to narrow children's educational opportunities and growth.

The findings of this study show that the long-term damage of sanctions manifests in ways such as the cancellation of remedial and supplementary classes, and even dropping out of school, and in the years to come, it will impose itself in the form of limited job and income opportunities on the generation that sits behind school desks today.
