🔘The way to tackle inequality
Angus Deaton, winner of the 2015 Nobel Prize in Economics, leads a team at the UK Institute for Fiscal Studies (IFS) focused on inequality. The fruit of this group's five years of study was published in April of this year; a book called "Challenging Inequalities," published by Princeton University Press, which has been widely welcomed. Martin Wolf says this book has five key conclusions about inequality:
1. Inequality of opportunity is inextricably linked to inequality of outcomes.
2. Redistribution of income and wealth matters, but so does the social status and dignity that comes from shaping one's own destiny.
3. The inequality-generating effects of policies related to trade, migration, and climate must be considered.
4. Seemingly fair market processes can have adverse consequences for inequality.
5. Economic growth still matters.
