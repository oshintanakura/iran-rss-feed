Education and health in the employment structure
In the European Union, the share of education and health in total employment is about 22 percent. In some Northern European countries, this ratio reaches 30 percent. More than 70 percent of jobs in these two sectors are held by women (approximately one-third of total female employment is in these two sectors).
In Turkey, Malaysia, Japan, South Korea, Algeria, and Tunisia, the share of these two sectors in total employment is about 11 percent, and in South America it averages between 11 and 12 percent.
The share of education and health in employment in Iran, Indonesia, Pakistan, and Bangladesh is about 8 percent. Apart from the role that education and health play in societal self-reliance, individual autonomy, citizenship, and social reproduction, even from an economic perspective, investment in these two sectors is highly effective in economic reproduction and growth. In Iran, input-output tables confirm this impact. Nevertheless, these two sectors in Iran face serious shortages in terms of financing, human resources, and infrastructure. This issue is particularly important given Iran's population dispersion and area. In this regard, this comparison might be interesting: the number of students in Iran is almost twice that of England, and the number of schools in Iran is more than four times. Iran's area is also about 12 times that of England. In Iran, more than 30 percent of students study in private and semi-private schools, while in England it is about 6 percent. However, the teaching staff of the two countries is almost equal.
In Iran, since the late 1980s, these two sectors have been the focus of government downsizing. Layers of the middle and upper classes also went along with these policies. But beyond the issue of government downsizing, the two institutions that are essential both for societal reproduction and economic growth were seen as surplus costs. According to one of the ministers of the first Hashemi government, some economists justified that these two sectors had the largest share of the government budget, and if only a little of these two were handed over to the private sector, the government's budget deficit would reach zero. He says we were "deceived" and accepted, but the more we handed over from these two sectors, the larger the budget deficit became.
Postscript: Education and health are two main areas of social investment, and there is great emphasis on investing in these two areas to face new risks. In successful experiences, due to the related and intertwined issues of these two areas, extensive job clusters have been formed at national and local levels.
