Footnote: I should also add that my observations pertain to a period after the end of the main war and should not be generalized to the past. Based on what I know from the days of the war, and in particular from the gatherings following the martyrdom of Ali Larijani, at that stage these gatherings were far more widespread and, in terms of class composition, generation, and social types, far more diverse as well. By the time I arrived in Tehran and began my field observations, the situation had changed considerably: the core of the gatherings appeared more homogeneous in terms of class, style of dress, religious rituals, and social type. Therefore, the description I offer in this section pertains to that specific period of observation, not to the social composition of the gatherings throughout the entire war. Put differently, the very shift in the composition of the crowd may itself be part of the evolution of this phenomenon in the interval between the days of the war and the period that followed. Nevertheless, distinguishing those three strata in the nighttime gatherings of the months after the forty-day war seems important.
