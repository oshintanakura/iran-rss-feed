✳️"The Night of the Dictator's Death"
We were always three: me, you, and "Him." "He" was always present—in the headscarves of women in movies at bedtime, in school textbooks, in the voices of the elegies that had taken over the streets. "He" was even more present in absences: in the absence of women's voices in our music, in the absence of watching a family football match at the stadium, in the absence of a homeland for millions of displaced people. Just as He was present in the absence of the thousands killed by His orders, at the celebration of His death.

And "The Night of the Dictator's Death" is the moment when He is no longer supposed to be everywhere. And it is precisely the morning after the dictator's death that, when we wake up, we know we are living in a different world—but we don't even know what that world looks like.

"He" is gone, but His shadow still looms over us. And all at once, we seem unable to forget that shadow. We have imagined this day so often as a wish that now the image of the world after His death keeps slipping away from our imagination.

"He" was present in everything and everywhere—all the surveillance cameras were His eyes. All the mobile phones were listening devices that seemed to be His ears. In a land that did not belong to "Him," He was everywhere. And His very presence was enough to keep us from talking more about His tomorrow. We had talked so little about His death that it had turned into a lump in the throat so large that we could no longer bear its joy.

"He" was gone, and the dance that had been aching inside us was turning into dance, into laughter, into a strange ambiguity in time.

"He" died, but war, violence, death—in a word, His shadow—still remains, still gaining strength. The bombs that drip from His words still take children's lives. The hatred He planted in this land is still gaining strength in the streets, and it is still hatred that spreads across the world.

We will hate "Him" forever, and no word can express that hatred. We still hate even His shadow—that shadow that seems to whisper from the depths of our hearts: "Are you sure the dictator is dead?"
