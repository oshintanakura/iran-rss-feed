#BookExcerpt Taken from "The Selfish Gene" by Richard Dawkins. This book presents Darwin's theory in simple language, focusing on the gene rather than the organism. Perhaps if I hadn't given this explanation, a reader unfamiliar with the author and book might think, after reading a few lines below, that it was a book about political science, war, or international competition: "The general answer to this question is that belligerence, besides its benefits, also has costs, apart from the obvious costs of time and energy. For example, suppose B and C are both my rivals, and I happen to confront B. Perhaps it would be reasonable for me, as a selfish individual, to try to eliminate him. But wait. C is both my rival and B's rival. By killing B, I actually benefit C as well, removing one of his rivals. Perhaps it would be better for me to let B live, because he might engage in competition with C, which would indirectly benefit me. The moral conclusion of this simple hypothetical example is that there is no obvious advantage in unthinkingly trying to kill rivals. In a large and complex competitive system, eliminating a rival is not necessarily beneficial; other rivals may benefit more from that killing than the individual himself." https://t.me/RezaEjtehadi
