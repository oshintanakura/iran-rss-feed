🔴How can we make the most of middle age? We shouldn't expect our past selves to have our present understanding. ✍Mostafa Malekian

Sometimes we look at the past and say: If I had spent those years doing something else, I would have a better life today. Why did I play so much as a child? Why did I pursue things in my youth that no longer matter to me today?

But in this judgment, we forget one thing: every period of life has its own demands. We cannot have had a childhood without having the interests and desires of childhood. We cannot have gone through youth and yet expect to have had our present maturity from the very beginning.

Part of our sorrow about the past comes from this very expectation — as if we wanted to have been as mature throughout our entire lives as we are today. Whereas it is precisely by going through those periods that we have reached this point.

▪️ Let us not see past mistakes as pure loss

Even a choice that we consider a mistake today may have brought us joys and friendships.

For example, we say, why did I pursue football instead of studying? But how much happiness has that same football given us? What friendships did it create that have stayed with us later in life?

If we only look at the costs of a choice and dismiss all the good things that came from it, we will not have a complete picture of the past.

▪️ No life is all benefit and no cost

Every way of life opens up possibilities before us and also carries costs. We cannot compare our lived life with an imaginary life that has all the good things and pays no price for them.

▪️ Let us seize the remaining opportunities

Not all opportunities are lost with the end of youth. There are still things we can begin, things we can learn, and pleasures we can experience.

If we spend today only on regret for yesterday, in a few more years, today will also become a new subject for regret.

Instead of only lamenting lost opportunities, let us treasure the opportunities not yet lost.
