🔴 Why do even good things sometimes make us feel bad? ✍️ Mostafa Malekian

We usually assume that if good things happen to us, we'll feel good too. But the pleasantness of an event, in itself, doesn't guarantee that we can handle it well.

We live in a world that can be hard on us in two ways: not everything goes the way we want, and we have no certainty about what's coming.

▪️ The world has no obligation to fulfill our desires

There's no reason all the world's events should conform to our preferences and wishes. Some events are pleasant and some unpleasant; even a single event can make one person happy and another miserable.

Rain is a source of joy for a farmer waiting to water his field; but for a launderer who has hung washed clothes out to dry in the sun, it spells trouble.

The rain is the same rain; what differs is its relation to our desires and circumstances. The world has neither sworn to be our enemy nor promised to always go along with what we want.

▪️ Even joys may show up uninvited

The difficulty isn't just that we dislike certain events; it's also that we don't know what will come, or when.

Suppose you love someone dearly, but they show up at your door unannounced. Despite being happy to see them, you might lose your composure and fail to receive them the way you'd like.

Loving a guest is not the same as being ready for their arrival.

The same holds for the good things in life: we may want something, yet not be prepared for its sudden arrival. As a result, our reaction may be ill-considered and create fresh problems.

▪️ The suffering of the event and the suffering of the reaction

Part of life's difficulty comes from events not matching our desires; another part can stem from how we face them.

If we expect life to always unfold according to our wishes and predictions, we'll be constantly caught off guard. And being caught off guard can push us into reactions that only deepen our suffering.

So we must prepare ourselves to live in a world that holds both adversity and uncertainty—so that we can meet what arrives uninvited with greater composure.

We can't control the arrival of every uninvited guest; but we can learn how to face them.
