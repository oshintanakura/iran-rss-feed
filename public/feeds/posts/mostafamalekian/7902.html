🔴Not every belief that keeps us alive is necessarily true. "Practical usefulness does not imply theoretical truth." ✍ Mostafa Malekian

🔹The fact that a belief helps us in practice, solves a problem, or even contributes to our survival does not by itself show that the belief corresponds to reality. Being useful is one thing; being true is another. You cannot infer the latter from the former.

A medicine may cure our headache, yet the theory on which the medicine was based may not be correct. The drug's effectiveness in curing does not by itself prove the correctness of the theory behind it.

The same distinction must be kept in mind in the discussion of evolution. If we accept Darwin's theory, we must note that the persistence of our beliefs, feelings, emotions, and desires does not necessarily mean they show us the truth; they may have persisted because they helped preserve the survival of our species.

That something ensures the survival of the human species is different from its expressing the truth. We may have held a belief, and that very belief kept our species alive; but from this one cannot conclude that the belief was certainly correct. It is even possible that awareness of a truth, rather than aiding our survival, endangers it.

🔹When people have said since ancient times that "the truth is bitter," one of its meanings is this: what is true is not always life-preserving for us or a guarantor of our continued existence.

🔹Therefore, even if a belief seems perfectly clear and self-evident to us and generation after generation has lived with it, this mere persistence and usefulness does not prove that it is true.

🔹From the fact that "this belief has helped our survival," one cannot conclude that "therefore this belief is true."
