🔴Can one be a materialist without denying God? | The difference between materialism and moral materialism ✍ Mostafa Malekian

Materialism comes in two very distinct forms: one is "philosophical materialism" and the other is "moral materialism" or "value materialism." To clarify the difference, we need to see what each one claims.

Who is a materialist in the philosophical sense?

Philosophical materialism is a view of the universe. Those who hold this view believe that nothing exists in the world unless it is the product of the exchange of matter and energy—that is, the product of matter converting into energy or energy converting into matter.

🔹But what about our mind and our mental states? Materialists hold two positions here: Some say that mind and mental states do not exist at all. If we ask why we believe they exist, they answer that this belief is a product of our "folk psychology"; it is within this psychology that something called mind and mental states appears to exist.

🔹Others say that mind and mental states do exist, but they are reducible to the same exchange of matter and energy. In a sense, mind and mental phenomena too seem to be wearing a disguise; otherwise, they too are simply material phenomena.

🔹With such a view, one can no longer believe in the existence of God or a non-material spirit and soul. This is philosophical materialism—a view that many philosophers have held throughout history.

But what is moral materialism?

Moral materialism has nothing to do with philosophical materialism at all; rather, it is a theory in axiology whose gist is this: the only thing that matters in life is wealth.

Here I use "wealth" in a sense that also includes money, property, and assets.

If you believe that in human life only one thing has value, and that is wealth, you are a moral materialist. Since value means having importance, it can be put more simply: in this view, only wealth is important and nothing else in life matters.

According to this view, wealth—for having a good and ideal life—is both a "necessary condition" and a "sufficient condition":

• Necessary condition means that unless you are wealthy, you will not have a good life.

• Sufficient condition means that if you have wealth, you no longer need anything else for a good life.

Most of us human beings, in most historical periods, have not been materialists in the first sense; but the majority of us are materialists in the second sense.
