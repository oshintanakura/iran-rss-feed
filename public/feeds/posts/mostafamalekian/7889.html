🔴 Ways to Cultivate Lasting, Real Pleasure in Midlife✍️Mostafa Malekian🔹If someone says, "I just want pleasure," how can we bring them to pleasure? We ask, do you want tea? They say no, I want pleasure. What about reading a book, traveling, or swimming? Again they say no, I just want pleasure. In that case, we have no way to fulfill their desire.But the moment they say, "I like reading books," a path opens up: they can read books and enjoy it.This is the very paradox of pleasure or happiness that John Stuart Mill puts forward: we must want something other than pleasure in order to reach pleasure. Pleasure is the result of attaining something we love.▪️ To be happy, we must attach our hearts to something beyond ourselvesConstant preoccupation with ourselves and with "what should I do to feel better?" can deprive us of the very thing we want. We must find an interest outside ourselves. This attachment takes two forms:First, loving other beings. When we love a person, their happiness makes us happy too. We do something for their comfort and we ourselves enjoy it as well. This affection can be directed toward humans, animals, plants, and other beings.Second, loving the work itself. Let us engage in work we enjoy while doing it; work whose process is dear to us. In that case, we do not postpone enjoyment until the result is reached.▪️ And let us also value the existential values of lifeFood, clothing, housing, sleep, and rest are necessary for the continuation of life; but life does not gain quality merely by meeting these needs. Alongside material values, four other categories of values can enrich life:▫️ Intellectual values: the pleasure of understanding, solving a problem, and finding an answer to a question.▫️ Aesthetic values: communion with nature, literature, and art; experiences that do not necessarily lengthen our lives, but give life quality.▫️ Moral values: humility, honesty, compassion, and love; qualities that can improve the quality of our living.▫️ Spiritual and transcendent values: a sense of oneness with beings, belonging to a transcendent being, and the connection between our own good and harm and that of others; of course, acceptance of this category is not a matter of consensus.To pass through midlife more smoothly, we must give more room to these existential values. Understanding, loving, artistic creation, and kindness, in midlife and beyond, can make our lives more flourishing.📚 Mostafa Malekian | A lecture about the book "Philosophical Guidance for Midlife"@mostafamalekian
