Margaret Hamilton, an American scientist and one of the pioneers of software engineering who played a key role in landing the first American astronauts on the Moon, has died at the age of 90. She led a team of experts who designed and developed the software used in NASA's Apollo space missions. MIT, confirming Hamilton's death, announced that she passed away on Wednesday, the eighth of Mehr 1405, corresponding to September 30, 2026. In a statement, the institute described Hamilton as one of the pioneers of computer science who played an important role in developing the field before software engineering became a widespread profession. Hamilton worked at MIT from 1959 until the mid-1970s and headed the software engineering division. The group she managed designed the guidance and control software for the Apollo 11 spacecraft, which played a decisive role in the historic landing of Neil Armstrong and Buzz Aldrin on the Moon in 1969. During this mission, the spacecraft's computer encountered a processing overload moments before landing, but the software designed by Hamilton's group was able to continue the landing operation by prioritizing tasks.
