US Energy Secretary Chris Wright and Venezuela's interim President Delcy Rodríguez signed an oil agreement in Caracas on Wednesday, under which the United States gains majority control over 65 billion barrels of Venezuela's oil reserves. This amount represents about one-fifth of Venezuela's vast oil reserves. US President Donald Trump has described the deal as "the biggest oil deal in the history of the world." Under the agreement, the US will have preferential access to 17 Venezuelan oil fields—facilities some of which were previously held by Russian and Chinese companies. Meanwhile, Chevron has also announced a separate $7 billion agreement to develop two other oil fields in the Orinoco Belt. Chevron says this investment could more than double its production in Venezuela within five years. The US Energy Secretary has predicted that Venezuela's oil production will exceed two million barrels per day by the end of this decade—about double the production level in January, when US forces overthrew Nicolás Maduro and Delcy Rodríguez took power. The agreement has also faced criticism, with detractors accusing Rodríguez's government of surrendering Venezuela's sovereignty over its oil resources.
