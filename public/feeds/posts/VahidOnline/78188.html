Source: @VahidOnline · 2026-09-02 13:22 UTC

The prices of foreign currencies in Iran set a new record on Wednesday morning, September 1, hours after the new round of U.S. attacks, with the euro, the European single currency, crossing the 255,000 toman mark for the first time. Currency rate websites, including "Nosan," reported the U.S. dollar at around 220,000 tomans. The UAE dirham also rose to over 60,000 tomans. The rise in foreign exchange rates in Iran's free market has intensified since the UAE announced cutting financial ties with Iran and the start of the U.S. economic pressure program called "Operation Economic Rejection." In the past two weeks, Iran's national currency has lost more than 10% of its value against major foreign currencies. On Wednesday, the price of the so-called "Emami" gold coin was reported at 224 million tomans, despite a global decline in gold prices. Abdolnaser Hemmati, the governor of the Central Bank, on September 1, rejected claims of a shortage of foreign exchange resources and the possibility of an economic collapse in Iran, saying the central bank is ready to supply up to two billion dollars to control the market.
