IRGC: We seized the second American unmanned submarine in the Strait of Hormuz

The Naval Force of the Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps claimed in a statement on Sunday, the fifth of Mehr, that it had identified and seized an American unmanned remotely operated underwater vehicle (UUV) in the Strait of Hormuz.

The IRGC statement said that its Naval Force personnel, in a "coordinated and complex operation" employing intelligence superiority and electronic warfare, trapped the subsurface vehicle, which was operating "to spy in the Strait of Hormuz."

The IRGC identified the submarine as a REMUS 600 and said it has taken it as a prize and it is now in the hands of IRGC Naval Force specialists so its data can be extracted and examined.

Media affiliated with the Islamic Republic also simultaneously released a video of the subsurface vehicle, presenting it as the "second" American UUV, or unmanned submarine, to have fallen into Iran's hands during the recent clashes in the Strait of Hormuz.

According to Iranian state media reports, this submarine is an autonomous underwater vehicle (UUV/AUV), and unlike a manned submarine, it carries no crew on board. This family of systems is used for missions including reconnaissance and countering naval mines, mapping the seabed, subsurface identification and surveillance, and gathering maritime intelligence.

Today's IRGC claim comes after, on the 17th of Shahrivar, the IRGC Naval Force had previously announced the seizure of another American subsurface vehicle near the entrance to the Strait of Hormuz.

CENTCOM said at the time that the submarine had stopped due to a technical malfunction and "did not contain sensitive information."
