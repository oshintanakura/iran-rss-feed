The dollar price in Iran's open market reached 213,700 tomans, and the dollar remittance rate hit 218,000 tomans. The British pound was also traded at 289,000 tomans. Today's figure marks the fourth consecutive record in four working days. The dollar closed at 206,400 tomans on Saturday, rose to 208,000 on Sunday, and reached 210,000 on Monday. As new records were set in the currency market, the head of the Central Bank said at the 36th Islamic Banking Conference that Iran does not face a currency shortage and that claims of economic collapse are false. In response to American officials' statements about Iran's lack of access to financial resources, Hemmati said: 'These claims are completely baseless. Unfrozen reserves, sustainable resources, and numerous oil and non-oil revenues are available to the Central Bank.' He added that the Central Bank allocated $500 million to the market last week and is now ready to inject up to $2 billion in currency if needed. Today's remarks by Hemmati differ from his previous stance. Last week, in a television interview, he had said: 'Our income from oil sales has dropped to zero; it's a fact that we are not exporting oil.' The shift in tone came after a message from Mojtaba Khamenei, the leader of the Islamic Republic, on the occasion of Government Week. In that message, he said that sometimes honestly expressing weaknesses helps the enemy and emphasized the need to 'explain and narrate Iran's power and strength.'
