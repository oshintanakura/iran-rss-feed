Shahrzad Shahbazi, one of the lawyers for the defendants in the "Aliakhani Square of Isfahan" case and the lawyer for Alireza Sepahi, has rejected the narrative put forward by state media, announcing that the charge of killing law enforcement officers was never communicated to any of the defendants in this case, nor did the court address this charge. So far, five of the defendants in this case have been executed, and seven others remain at risk of execution. The lawyer's statements came in response to the publication of an interview in state media in which a woman, introduced as the wife of one of the killed officers, accuses the defendants of killing and burning officers. The interview was published after criticism mounted over the carrying out of executions, particularly the execution of Alireza Sepahi. Shahbazi also questioned the presence of this woman at the site of the executions, asking: when the case did not involve qisas or a personal right, on what basis was she allowed to be present at the site where the sentence was carried out? In a message posted on Instagram on Saturday, 18 Mehr 1405, Shahbazi wrote: "The charge of killing has under no circumstances been communicated to any of the defendants in the Aliakhani Square and Shohada Square case." She emphasized that the allegation of "burning officers in connection with premeditated murder" was neither addressed in court nor included among the charges in the verdict. Shahbazi cited the Revolutionary Court's lack of jurisdiction over this charge as the reason it did not take up the matter of killing. The Aliakhani Square case was opened after four Special Unit officers were killed during the protests of 18 Dey 1404 in Isfahan. State media had previously published videos of the defendants' confessions during interrogations, crime scene reconstructions, and trial sessions in Branch One of the Isfahan Revolutionary Court — confessions that human rights bodies consider forced and invalid. Alireza Sepahi was executed in the early hours of Monday, 13 Mehr. Mai Sato, the United Nations Special Rapporteur on human rights in Iran, in reaction to the execution, had reported serious concerns about the torture of defendants and the lack of a fair trial in this case. According to Sato, the sentence was carried out while Sepahi's lawyer was still working to have the case reviewed. Sepahi had previously also been on the verge of public execution, but was transferred to the hospital after a heart attack.
