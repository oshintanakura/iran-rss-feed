The Financial Times reported on Friday, September 4, that disagreements between the United States and the Islamic Republic of Iran have increasingly focused on the future of the Strait of Hormuz; because the Trump administration has rejected a return to the Islamabad Memorandum of Understanding, while Tehran seeks to revive this agreement as a basis for reducing tensions and resuming oil transit through the Strait of Hormuz. According to this report, diplomatic efforts to bring the parties back to the Islamabad Memorandum, which included a cessation of military actions, reopening the Strait of Hormuz, and starting more comprehensive negotiations, have been met with Washington's opposition. The US now demands a new and more comprehensive agreement that includes Iran's nuclear file in addition to the status of the Strait of Hormuz. In contrast, Masoud Pezeshkian has emphasized that his country is ready to fulfill its commitments as soon as the US returns to its obligations under the interim agreement. However, Washington is counting on the leverage of field pressure and, by strengthening its military presence, mine sweeping, and creating safe routes, is trying to prove that Iran can no longer use the Strait of Hormuz as a pressure card on the energy market.@VahidOOnLine📡 @VahidOnline
