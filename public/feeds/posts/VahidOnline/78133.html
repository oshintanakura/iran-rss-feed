Global shipping monitoring companies say two supertankers carrying Saudi Arabian oil were hit by unknown projectiles within minutes of each other late Monday, August 31, while passing through the Strait of Hormuz. According to Reuters, the Greek maritime security firm Marinex announced on Tuesday, September 1, that the supertanker 'Sider,' carrying Saudi crude oil and flying the Saudi flag, was struck by unknown projectiles at approximately 19:52 GMT on Monday, about 16 nautical miles northeast of Khasab, Oman. The maritime security company Vanguard Tech also said the tanker 'Senegal Prosperity,' flying the Liberian flag, was hit by three unknown projectiles minutes later, about 17 nautical miles east of Khasab. Earlier, the UK Maritime Trade Operations had reported the attack on this tanker, saying three projectiles struck it as it exited the Strait of Hormuz. According to reports, the crews of both tankers are safe, and both vessels have stopped within a short distance of each other. Data from Kepler shows that each of these tankers had loaded 2 million barrels of Saudi crude oil from the port of Juaymah in the Persian Gulf last week.
