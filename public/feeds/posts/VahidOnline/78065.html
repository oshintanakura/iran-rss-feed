The Trump administration has told mediators that it has no desire to return to the terms of the initial June agreement with the Islamic Republic. This stance has hindered the renewed efforts of Qatar, Oman, and Pakistan to revive talks between Washington and Tehran. The Wall Street Journal reported on Thursday, citing informed sources, that the Trump administration has repeatedly conveyed this position to mediators. The initial agreement, brokered by Pakistan, sought the reopening of the Strait of Hormuz, the start of talks on the Islamic Republic's nuclear program, and an end to the war. In return, sanctions relief and Tehran's access to frozen assets were envisioned. According to the Wall Street Journal, Trump has now prioritized economic pressure on the Islamic Republic and is prepared to wait to see the outcome of this policy. Anna Kelly, a White House spokesperson, also said no negotiations with the Islamic Republic are underway or planned, and the naval blockade and "economic exclusion operation" will continue. This report was published as Pakistan's army chief, Asim Munir, traveled to Tehran earlier this week for talks. Oman's foreign minister has also held discussions with Islamic Republic officials to reach an understanding on the shipping route through the Strait of Hormuz. Qatar's prime minister also met with Islamic Republic officials in Tehran on Thursday. The Wall Street Journal wrote that the dispute over how to interpret the June agreement and the conditions for reopening the Strait of Hormuz has made it difficult to reach a framework for resuming talks.
