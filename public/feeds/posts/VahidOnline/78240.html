The price of the dollar in Iran's open market on Saturday, September 5, rose with another jump to 228,000 tomans, and the euro also surpassed 263,000 tomans. Websites that display unofficial currency rates in Iran have also announced the price of the British pound at 307,000 tomans and the UAE dirham at over 62,000 tomans. This renewed increase occurred just one day after Abdolnaser Hemmati, the governor of the Central Bank of Iran, rejected a serious shortage of foreign currency for imports and attributed the sharp decline of Iran's national currency to increased demand for "precautionary, speculative purposes and capital flight." The dollar price crossed the 200,000-toman mark at the beginning of September and has been continuously rising over the past two weeks. This is while Hemmati had said last week that Iran has "enough" foreign currency and that the central bank is ready to inject up to two billion dollars into the market if needed, but these statements have not prevented the continued rise in the exchange rate.@VahidHeadline📡 @VahidOnline
