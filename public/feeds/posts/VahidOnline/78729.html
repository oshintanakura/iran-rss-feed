Varisha Moradi's lawyers announced on Wednesday, 22 Mehr, that Branch 15 of the Tehran Revolutionary Court — which had previously sentenced this political prisoner held in Evin to execution on charges of "membership in a rebel group" — insisted on its previous death sentence despite the Supreme Court's request to examine "deficiencies in the investigation."

Amir Sajjadi and Keyvan Azizi, Varisha Moradi's lawyers, speaking to "Emtedad," confirmed this news and stated that on Wednesday, 22 Mehr, they visited Branch 15 of the Revolutionary Court and received the verdict issued by this branch, and according to the notified verdict, they have 20 days to appeal to the Supreme Court.

According to them, this verdict was issued in a situation where the previous verdict of Branch 15 sentencing Varisha Moradi to death had been overturned by the reviewing branch in the Supreme Court after serious doubts were raised about the investigation; yet the aforementioned branch, without conducting the investigation requested by the Supreme Court, merely changed the title of the charge and once again sentenced Ms. Moradi to execution on the charge of membership in a rebel group.

Amir Sajjadi, stating that "in the lawyers' view, there are no sufficient and solid grounds for attributing such a charge to Varisha Moradi," emphasized: "Ms. Moradi has absolutely not been involved in any operations against the Islamic Republic, and her activities were solely related to her fight against ISIS in northern Syria."

Keyvan Azizi also, emphasizing that Varisha Moradi's lawyers will appeal within the legal deadline, expressed hope that "the Supreme Court, as in the previous instance, will give sufficient attention to the defenses and objections."

Varisha Moradi, who is currently imprisoned in Evin Prison, was arrested on 10 Mordad 1402 on the outskirts of Sanandaj, and on 5 Dey of the same year, upon completion of the interrogation stages, was transferred from the Ministry of Intelligence detention center known as Ward 209 of Evin Prison to the women's ward of this prison.

She was sentenced to execution on 20 Aban 1403 by Branch 15 of the Tehran Revolutionary Court on charges of "baghy" (armed uprising against the government). In an open letter from prison, she revealed that during her detention she was subjected to physical and psychological torture, death threats, and gender-based violence.
