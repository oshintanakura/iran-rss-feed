Qalibaf: The rules of the game have changed and the era of proportionate response has ended. The Speaker of the Islamic Consultative Assembly (Majlis), on Monday, September 6, a day after the US attack on several Iranian oil tankers in the Persian Gulf, said the era of "proportionate responses" has come to an end. He simultaneously acknowledged the existence of economic problems in the country. Mohammad Bagher Qalibaf, in remarks at an open session of the Majlis, threatened: "Any aggression against Iran's interests and security will receive a faster, heavier, and more painful response." Qalibaf, who is the Islamic Republic's senior negotiator in post-ceasefire talks with the US, said in another part of his Monday speech: "Severe fluctuations in the currency exchange rate, inflation, unemployment, and market management are fundamental challenges that have put serious pressure on people's livelihoods." He added: "Alongside the military arena, today our main battle is in the arena of production and the livelihood of the people." These remarks come a day after the price of the dollar in Iran's free market rose to near 228,000 tomans, and on the other hand, official statistics also indicate a sharp increase in inflation in recent months. Ali Madanizadeh, Iran's Minister of Economy, also said on Monday that Tehran's reaction to the intensification of US economic pressure is "economic resistance alongside economic reforms," and rejected the view that sanctions will change Iran's course. Referring to statements by senior officials of Donald Trump's administration about the US action to cut Iran's ties with the global economy, he said: "The idea that one can change the decisions of a nation by putting pressure on its economy is a mistake." Iran's Minister of Economy added: "The responsibility for reforming Iran's economy lies with the Iranian government and people, not the US Treasury Department." This comes while the US Treasury Secretary simultaneously announced that the combination of a naval blockade and extensive sanctions has severely limited oil exports and the Islamic Republic of Iran's access to its revenues.
