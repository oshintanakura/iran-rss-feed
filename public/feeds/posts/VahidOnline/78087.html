The Washington Post, citing informed sources, reported that several senior U.S. military officials have warned Pete Hegseth, the U.S. Secretary of Defense, that continuing large-scale military operations against the Islamic Republic is unsustainable and would weaken the U.S. military's ability to counter other threats, including defending U.S. territory. According to these individuals, these warnings—which the chiefs of the Army, Navy, and Air Force, along with the four-star commanders responsible for U.S. military operations in Europe, Asia, and Latin America, presented to Hegseth in the August 14 version of the "Secretary of Defense's Orders Book"—are part of a classified document. According to the report, given Trump's emphasis that the military option remains on the table, the U.S. Central Command (CENTCOM), which is responsible for managing the war with the Islamic Republic, has kept more than 50,000 troops on standby for months to act if the President orders further strikes. According to informed sources, the August 14 version of the Secretary of Defense's Orders Book stipulated that some forces deployed in the Middle East would remain in the region until the end of September, and the missions of others would be extended until 2027. The possibility of further extensions of these deployments prompted military commanders to openly raise their concerns. According to these sources, senior commanders of European Command, Pacific Command, and U.S. Southern Command, along with the senior Navy commander, have recorded a "non-concurrence" opinion in this document; meaning they do not agree with the Secretary of Defense's order to extend their forces' deployments, but they will execute it. @VahidOOnLine📡 @VahidOnline
