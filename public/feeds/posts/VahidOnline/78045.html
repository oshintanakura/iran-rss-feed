Bloomberg, reviewing Turkey's gas imports, assessed that Washington's new warning about economic sanctions on countries dealing with Tehran has put Turkey—a key US ally and Iran's third-largest trading partner—facing the challenge of cutting gas imports from Iran. Last year, Turkey supplied 13% of its imported gas (7.7 billion cubic meters) from Iran, making Iran its fourth-largest energy supplier after Russia, Azerbaijan, and the US. Despite the expiration of the 25-year contract at the end of July, gas receipts from Iran have continued. US Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent has stated that any country continuing economic relations with the Islamic Republic of Iran will be targeted for sanctions, and Donald Trump is in direct consultation with world leaders. This likely includes Washington's contact with Recep Tayyip Erdogan. Bloomberg assessed that Erdogan, who is heading to Washington next month and needs America's green light for major military purchases including F-35 and F-16 fighter jets, is unlikely to seek to anger Trump. According to experts, if Iranian gas is cut off, Ankara could compensate for the shortfall by increasing imports of more expensive liquefied natural gas (LNG)—especially from the US—and relying on its filled reserves. @VahidOOnLine📡 @VahidOnline
