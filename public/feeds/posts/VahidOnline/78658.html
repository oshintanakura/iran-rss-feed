Reuters reported on Thursday, 16 Mehr, that the number of ships transiting the Strait of Hormuz has fallen to its lowest level in more than two months, following a surge in attacks on oil tankers last week. Citing data and analysis from the analytics firm Kpler, Reuters reported that only 7 commercial vessels passed through the Strait of Hormuz on Tuesday — the lowest figure since the first of Mordad. Crude oil transit through the strait also dropped 27 percent from the wartime peak the previous week, falling to at least 10.1 million barrels per day, equivalent to 74 percent of the pre-war level. According to Kpler analysts, most of this decline is attributable to ship-to-ship transfers of cargoes in the Sea of Oman. According to Reuters, however, exports from the coasts of the Sea of Oman and the Red Sea rose to 6.7 million barrels per day — more than double the pre-war level — helping to offset the reduced supply through the Strait of Hormuz. Kpler data shows the number of transiting ships rose to 10 on Wednesday, but remained far below the more than 20 vessels seen on Sunday and Monday. The report comes after attacks on oil tankers passing through the Strait of Hormuz last week reached their highest weekly level since the start of the Iran war. Before the war began on the ninth of Esfand last year, roughly 125 large commercial ships — including oil tankers, gas carriers, bulk carriers, and container ships — passed through the Strait of Hormuz daily.
