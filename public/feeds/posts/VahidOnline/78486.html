A senior official of the Islamic Republic has said Tehran has proposed that if the United States reduces military pressure and takes initial steps to end the blockade of Iran's ports, it will reopen the Strait of Hormuz within seven days and return to negotiations with Washington. The Kyodo news agency reported on Tuesday, 31 Shahrivar 1405, citing this official, whose name was not disclosed, that the proposal was conveyed to the U.S. government through intermediaries and is part of Tehran's new effort to revive negotiations with Washington. Under the proposal, the Islamic Republic is seeking to resume negotiations aimed at reaching an agreement for a "permanent end to hostilities" between Iran and the United States. The official said that in the first phase, Tehran expects Washington to show signs of readiness to return to negotiations and to begin taking measures to end the military blockade of Iran's ports and halt military operations related to the Strait of Hormuz. If these steps are taken, the Islamic Republic is prepared to open the shipping route through the Strait of Hormuz within seven days and return to the negotiating table. The official stressed that America must demonstrate its "seriousness and commitment" for diplomacy to move forward. Kyodo reported that Tehran's new proposal has been approved by "Mojtaba Khamenei," the Leader of the Islamic Republic, and the Supreme National Security Council. The Iranian official did not specify whether the proposal means Tehran is backing away from part of the seven conditions previously put forward for negotiations and the reopening of the Strait of Hormuz. According to Kyodo's report, the Supreme National Security Council had decided on 25 Mordad that if America does not end the blockade of Iran's ports within 45 days, the Islamic Republic would reserve the option of attacking American forces again. That deadline is now approaching. At the same time, a senior Iranian official has told "Reuters" that the Islamic Republic's delegation at the United Nations General Assembly in New York has full authority to revive diplomatic talks with America, and the details of a possible agreement can be discussed through mediating countries in New York.
