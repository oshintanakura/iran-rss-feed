Oman's Foreign Minister announced the postponement of a meeting between Iran and the Persian Gulf and regional countries — a meeting that was set to be held on Monday, 23 Shahrivar, in Salalah, Oman, focusing on the situation in the Strait of Hormuz. Badr Al-Busaidi, Oman's Foreign Minister, wrote on Sunday, 22 Shahrivar, on X that the meeting was postponed "in order to reach consensus." He stressed that Oman remains committed to strengthening dialogues that contribute to "sustainable stability and cooperation in the region." Abbas Araghchi, the Foreign Minister of the Islamic Republic, had previously said he would attend the eight-party meeting of foreign ministers from the coastal countries of the Persian Gulf and the Sea of Oman in Salalah on Monday. The meeting was supposed to discuss a plan by Iran and Oman to establish a mechanism for the safe passage of ships through the Strait of Hormuz. The postponement comes as the United States had previously emphasized that it would not take part in negotiations related to the Strait of Hormuz and would focus any direct talks with the Islamic Republic on the nuclear file. American officials have told regional countries that Washington will not negotiate over the situation in the Strait of Hormuz, and that the main subject of any potential negotiations with Tehran should be the Islamic Republic's nuclear program. Marco Rubio, the U.S. Secretary of State, had also previously said that the Strait of Hormuz should not be under the control of the Islamic Republic, and that the United States would act to guarantee the security of shipping along this route. In contrast, the Islamic Republic and Oman have sought to bring regional countries into dialogue about a mechanism for ship passage through the Strait of Hormuz.
