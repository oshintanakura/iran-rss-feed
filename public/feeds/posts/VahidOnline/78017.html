Field Marshal Asim Munir, the Chief of Army Staff of Pakistan, arrived in Tehran on Monday. Pakistan's Interior Minister Mohsin Naqvi is accompanying him on the trip to the Iranian capital. The Pakistani army issued a statement saying that this senior military commander's visit to Tehran "will focus on Islamabad's efforts to promote regional peace and stability, and negotiations with Iranian officials on strengthening peace efforts and finding a peaceful, sustainable, and comprehensive solution to resolve Middle East conflicts." IRIB News reported that Asim Munir will meet with senior officials of the Islamic Republic. @VahidOOnLineReuters, citing several Pakistani officials, reported that Asim Munir, Pakistan's army chief, spoke by phone with Donald Trump last week before his trip to Tehran. Three Pakistani sources told Reuters that the call took place a few days before Munir was expected to travel to Tehran on Monday for talks with Islamic Republic officials. According to Reuters, this previously unreported call took place at a time when the US has announced it will impose broad economic sanctions against the Islamic Republic and its trading partners. The report also stated that Pakistan's army chief is expected to meet on Monday with people close to Mojtaba Khamenei. Reuters wrote that tensions between the US and the Islamic Republic are expected to be one of the main topics of this visit. Another source in the Pakistani government also said: "Munir is also expected to discuss the recent attacks by the Islamic Republic-affiliated Houthis on Saudi Arabia, Pakistan's ally." @VahidOOnLineIsmail Baghaei, the spokesman for the Islamic Republic's Ministry of Foreign Affairs, announced on Monday that Badr al-Busaidi, Oman's Minister of Foreign Affairs, will travel to Tehran on Tuesday. According to IRIB News, Baghaei told reporters that Busaidi will meet with Abbas Araghchi, the Islamic Republic's Foreign Minister, in Tehran. Following the US and Israeli attack and the closure of the Strait of Hormuz, the Islamic Republic has begun negotiations with Oman to define a new legal regime for the Strait of Hormuz. Tehran, Muscat, and Doha report progress in these negotiations; however, US President Donald Trump threatened last week that he would bomb Oman if it obstructs the path of a "deal" between Tehran and Washington. Al-Busaidi mediated two rounds of negotiations between the Islamic Republic and the United States last year.
