Vance: Iran must meaningfully reduce enrichment; extent of Pezeshkian and Araghchi's authority unclear

🔸JD Vance, the Vice President of the United States, says the Iranian government must "meaningfully" reduce its uranium enrichment capacity to end the war, and that the United States will not settle for verbal promises in negotiations with the Islamic Republic — it demands concrete action from Tehran.

🔸Mr. Vance, in an interview with Reuters news agency published in the early hours of Wednesday, 15 Mehr, also said the United States is in contact and negotiating with Masoud Pezeshkian, the President of Iran, and Abbas Araghchi, the Foreign Minister, but it is not clear to Washington to what extent these two officials have decision-making authority within Iran's current power structure.

🔸His remarks come as Tehran and Washington have exchanged proposals in recent weeks to end the war and fully reopen the Strait of Hormuz, but the two sides still have a fundamental disagreement over the scope of negotiations and the nuclear issue.

🔸The Vice President of the United States, in response to Reuters' question about Washington's conditions, addressed the officials of the Islamic Republic, saying: "If you don't want a nuclear weapon, then why do you need 60 percent enriched fuel? And if you want to demonstrate your commitment to not building a nuclear weapon, don't produce highly enriched fuel. This is a very basic and decisive issue."

🔸He added: "I think if they want to demonstrate their commitment to not building a nuclear weapon, they must take meaningful action regarding their enrichment capacity."

🔸Mr. Vance at the same time emphasized that America is still ready to reach an agreement, but such an agreement must include specific and practical concessions from Iran regarding the nuclear program.

🔸He said: "We are not going to trade words for actions."
