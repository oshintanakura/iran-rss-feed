Scott Bessent, the U.S. Treasury Secretary, said in an interview with Newsmax that the United States is not necessarily seeking the collapse of the Islamic Republic of Iran, although internal developments and a popular uprising are possible. He also pointed to the personal risks facing Iran's leaders and military commanders as pressures increase. Bessent dismissed Iran's claims of control over the Strait of Hormuz, saying that with about 17 million barrels of oil passing through yesterday, Iran's control over the strait is meaningless. He also denied reports of mines or two ships hitting mines in the Strait of Hormuz and accused the media of quickly republishing Iran's false claims. The U.S. Treasury Secretary, noting China's continued purchase of Iranian oil, emphasized that only about 30 million barrels of Iran's oil remain on the water, and these reserves will soon run out. During his speech at the G20 economic forum yesterday, Bessent also stressed that economic pressures will either lead to divisions within the IRGC and possibly the people confronting them, or Tehran officials will decide to return to the negotiating table.
