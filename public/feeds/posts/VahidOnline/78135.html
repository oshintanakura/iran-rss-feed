Simultaneously with the spread of news about the presence of "hijab enforcers" in Tehran's Grand Bazaar and statements by some officials of the Islamic Republic regarding the need for "mandatory hijab," images have been published of banners installed in various cities of Iran in which women are threatened with judicial punishment if they do not observe the hijab as required by the government.
In these banners, reference is made to the note of Article 638 of the Islamic Penal Code, stating: "The presence of women without Islamic hijab in public places and view is a crime and carries a prison sentence."
The installed banner also refers to Articles 7 and 9 of the so-called "Support for Enjoiners of Good and Forbidders of Evil" law. In the explanation of Article 7, it is written that individuals who create obstacles against "enjoining good and forbidding evil" are not eligible for sentence reduction or suspension.
