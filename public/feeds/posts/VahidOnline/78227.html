The New York Post reported on Thursday, September 3, citing a senior regional official, that Oman has rejected the Islamic Republic's proposal for jointly receiving service fees from commercial ships transiting the Strait of Hormuz. The official said Muscat did not even agree to voluntarily receiving environmental and security service fees from ships. A US official also told the New York Post that the terms of the proposed agreement between the Islamic Republic and Oman for revenue sharing have not been finalized.

These remarks came after Hossein Mohebbi, a spokesman for the Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps, had earlier announced that Tehran and Muscat had reached an agreement in this regard.

Reuters reported on July 29 that Oman had presented a plan backed by Persian Gulf countries to the Islamic Republic, according to which the management of the Strait of Hormuz would be conducted regionally, and shipping companies could voluntarily pay amounts to cover the costs of navigation, environmental protection, and search and rescue operations. Oman had previously also opposed the mandatory collection of fees from ships transiting this waterway.
