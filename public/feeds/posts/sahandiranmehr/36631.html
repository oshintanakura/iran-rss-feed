"Feeling the Sorrow of Others" in a Tale by Saadi

In his tale of the Damascus famine, Saadi speaks of a man who is not himself in straitened circumstances, yet the suffering of the hungry robs him of pleasure. Can one, in a time of war, economic hardship, and insecurity, be concerned about the suffering of others and still carry on with life?

In this episode, inspired by Saadi's tale, we turn to the meaning of empathy in difficult days; we speak about the necessity of compassion and of lessening the suffering of others, and we reflect on how the flood of images of suffering on social media can gradually numb us. In this installment we will also seek answers to these questions: Is happiness in a time of hardship a sign of indifference, and how can discontent become a criterion for judging others?
