🔶This film is one of the most valuable documents of Iran's contemporary history. It shows the speech of the first Iranian at the first meeting of the United Nations in January 1946 in London. The speaker is the representative of the Iranian government, Seyyed Hassan Taghizadeh. Many consider Taghizadeh to be Iran's first true intellectual. He was a multifaceted figure. He went on to serve as ambassador, parliamentarian, minister, and president of the Senate. Alongside politics, he was also a distinguished scholar and thinker. During his time in the political arena, Taghizadeh influenced many intellectuals. Many, moved by his ideas, turned toward Western civilization. However, ill-wishers branded him "Westoxicated" and leveled the worst accusations against him. But he loved his homeland and was beholden to no one. More than a prominent and influential politician, he was an influential intellectual. He was the first politician to boldly undertake "self-criticism" and critique his own behavior and words: "When the matter of Atabak's nomination for premiership became the talk of the town, I used everything at my disposal—from the sting of my tongue to the poison of my pen—to bring him down before his premiership. In my speeches, I turned him into an absolute evil who was coming to tear down the constitution. I portrayed all his services as treason. I made such a demon of him that the blood of listeners younger than myself would boil, and they would lose control and resort to violent acts. I need not have issued a decree for terrorism to be held responsible for it; to the same extent that I make the atmosphere heated and radical, I bear moral responsibility for the consequences. When we turn our political opponent into a monster, we must be aware of our intellectual and moral responsibility."... "All my life I am paying the price for when Mohammad Ali Shah came through the door of reconciliation with the constitutionalists, I dug in my heels and opposed it."... "In the first period of my life, when I rose up in strife and aggression, I made mistakes, and I had the courage to admit this mistake." ... "The constitutionalists did not even shy away from uttering insults against the honor and reputation of the Shah in the newspapers, and the Shah's response was to file a complaint with the court, but not only was the Shah's complaint not addressed, other constitutionalists attempted an unsuccessful assassination of the Shah. Sixty years on from those days, I cannot hide my regret over the failure to reach understanding with Mohammad Ali Shah" (Andisheh-ye Pouya, Issue 39).
