✔️Returning to the homeland and fear of a rift in the opposition! ✍🏻Sahand Iranmehr
The return of an Iranian who has not taken up arms against his country, has not called for violence, and has not supported foreign aggression against Iran should, in principle, be a matter of course—not a political concession. If even a modicum of rationality were applied, thousands of compatriots at home who harbor the unavoidable dream of emigration could also stay put.
That the trouble-free return of a handful of compatriots today comes as a surprise to some is the result of conditions that have driven a wedge between the Iranian and his homeland—a wedge that has provided the perfect ecosystem for all kinds of crises to take root and, consequently, for those who profit from them to thrive. So if this fledgling and "still trembling sapling of return" is displeasing to some, it may be because they would rather not see even a single knot in this tangled skein of "Iran's situation" come undone. It is these knots, piled one upon another, that absolve reason from effort, keep the cauldron of rage simmering on its own, and turn blood into a commodity for trade and for garnering support among this lot.
If the fly flees the dunghill, these too abhor conflict and prefer union over separation; because in their view, if a knot is to be untied, it must first receive the blessing of a Homa's shadow, and portraits must dance upon the shoulders of rage and destruction, and a brother must hide a blade in his sleeve aimed at the jugular of his own kin; for in a politics built on elimination, reconciliation and return are sometimes more dangerous than defeat itself, and they stoke the fires of crisis more than ever.
In short, don't be too surprised if they say: "These permissions are meant to create a rift in the opposition!" And don't be astonished if, one distant day, when the Homa's shadow falls upon the nation and the bitter times have passed, this lost lot mourns the dissipation of the opposition and beats their heads in despair!
These returns are, of course, good; even better is when all these differences and even hair-pulling squabbles take place within Iran, under the red line of "Iran." But sit back and watch how, in the days ahead, the knife and cheese—both inside and outside, with seemingly opposing edges—will secretly join hands to stitch the earth to the sky for this trembling, fledgling endeavor. Freeing Iran from its troubles has long since, for some, ceased to be a struggle for change and become a furnace to fan the flames within; and that is how, instead of thinking about solving its problems, they think about keeping them unsolved—and we've seen that if they catch wind of a "Truman Plus" that could wrap things up, they'll cheer for that too.
