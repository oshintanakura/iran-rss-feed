✔️Ali Karimi and Three Thought-Provoking Frames ✍🏻Sahand Iranmehr
Frame One: Ali Karimi began his so-called political career with insults, and now the very same people who cheered him on are busy insulting him.
Frame Two: A few days ago, I was watching a video of some of those residing in America who call themselves the "Immortal Guard," engaged in so-called military drills in the woods, flaunting their weapons and mocking those who talk about reading books and awareness.
Frame Three: Not long ago, one of these individuals, referring to my novels, commented on a piece I had written: "Go write your novel."
Final Word: In my first novel, "Report of the Last Summer," I wrote through the voice of a character who was once a guerrilla and, decades later, looks back at those memories with a cautionary eye: "We thought we could leave the violence we wielded in the streets at the doorstep and live differently at home." Today's saga of Ali Karimi is one manifestation of a situation that is hardly strange or unpredictable for someone who has read a few books and novels instead of flaunting a weapon or the sharpness of juicy insults. Sometimes, to escape the violence done to oneself, one transfers that very violence to another. To escape humiliation, one humiliates. To avoid being a victim, one creates victims. To avoid being under someone's thumb, one raises one's own hand against another—and when one becomes the initiator of this path, one becomes its victim. Seeing these people and their experiences shows that knowing, reading, and understanding this futile method is no longer a decorative virtue; it is a way to avoid falling into this cycle and not to be surprised when the equation is inverted.
