🔸️Ahmad asked: Are you back to writing again? At this age, with all these ailments, severe weakness, and failing eyesight, why do you shorten your life? Everything you have said and written so far, if read and heard, would suffice to awaken every sleeper and alert every conscious mind—yet you see that in the heart of the Iranian, it has had no more effect than raindrops on solid rock. And instead of acceptance and acknowledgment, you hear accusations of madness and weak faith? This is itself a wrong you do to yourself and your family.
🔸️A few days ago, in a gathering, your book was passed from hand to hand; one praised its binding, another described its calligraphy, a third admired its maps. One of the influential figures, clearing his throat in the inherited manner of our dear, undeserving leaders, said: I've heard the author of these reasonable absurdities is well-off and has means, but they say he's deranged. Whatever he says and writes is about reforming the homeland's flaws. Isn't there anyone to ask him: Good man, what has happened to our homeland, with its heavenly fruits and the aroma of bread found nowhere else, that it needs reforming?
🔸️Someone from the sidelines said: I've met him; you can't call him deranged. The poor fellow is unwell and suffers from severe self-importance. He approves of no one and no class in Iran. When I spoke of the "center," he said it is lawless and disorderly. When I asked about the governors, he said they are tyrants, oppressors, and bribe-takers. When I brought up the scribes, he said they draw the loops of "mim" and "noon" beautifully but know no geometry and have not studied arithmetic—may the devil take their calculations. When I asked about the seminary students, he said they know Yaghma the lyric poet well. When I inquired about the clerics, he said those in the holy shrines are free of greed, need no luxuries, and are content with cheese and onions instead of undue partridge and francolin stews—they are our spiritual fathers and, as successors to the Imams, must be obeyed. But most of those in Iran are hoarders and monopolists; they love chaos, relish the rabble's uproar, and worship the sound of clogs. They have annual incomes of thirty to fifty thousand tomans from their estates... When I asked about the merchants, he said they are frauds; apart from promoting the sale of foreign goods or hoarding grains, this class brings no benefit to the land or the nation...
🔸️After relaying this news, Ahmad sighed and said: "These words of mine suffice for you to consider all your efforts for this ignorant nation as blown to the wind, washed away by water, and burned by fire"...
🔸️I listened and became more despondent than Ahmad himself, wondering why he lets others' opinions dictate whether he acts or refrains.
