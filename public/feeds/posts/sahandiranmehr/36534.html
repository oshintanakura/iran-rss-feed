What do 23 votes in favor and 3 votes against mean? Sahand Iranmehr. Numbers are tantalizing because they evoke a sense of undeniable decisiveness and certainty, but the famous saying "statistics speak" is not entirely true, at least in political analysis. The 23 votes in favor, 3 votes against, and 8 abstentions show that Iran has been left in the minority at the IAEA Board of Governors and a resolution against it has been passed. The meaning of these statistics is that the majority of the Board of Governors has not sided with Iran's position. This fact should not be downplayed, nor should one, for instance, flaunt the negative votes of China, Russia, and Niger to compensate. However, at the same time, this vote does not mean that the matter is over and that new sanctions, this time from the United Nations, are on the way. It is true that the Board of Governors can report the case to the Security Council, but the Security Council has its own specific rules, because now Russia and China, in addition to their negative votes, are permanent members with veto power. Just as Iran being in the minority there was a bitter reality, here in the Security Council, one should not romanticize the negative votes of Moscow, Beijing, and Niger either, considering the veto right. The negative votes of Russia, China, and Niger do not mean that they endorse all of Iran's nuclear policies or consider the differences between Tehran and the Agency to be fabricated. They oppose the politicized process of dealing with Iran's nuclear issue, a path that seeks to drag the case to the Security Council more than it serves the Agency's goals. This is also why China and Russia, despite being in the minority in Vienna, see their minority status fade in New York where the veto power works. If we want to face the issue realistically, we must accept that 23 votes in favor are, in any case, a serious wake-up call, and even if it is proven to be a political move, the essence of the issue does not change. Nevertheless, the West is also far from a complete victory. Taking the case from Vienna to New York is one thing, and obtaining a binding decision in the Security Council is another. The majority formed today in the Board of Governors will not necessarily be repeated in the Security Council, but how far this organized effort based on global consensus will continue, how far the brakes of China and Russia will work, or how far other variables will keep these two aligned with Iran, is something that, in a wise approach, one cannot gamble on too much. @sahandiranmehr
