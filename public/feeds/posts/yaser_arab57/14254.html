♦️ Trees that become victims of war! ♦️📚@darvishnameh✍️ Mangroves, including Avicennia and Rhizophora species, help absorb carbon and produce oxygen several times more than any forest habitat we know, and in addition, they are highly effective in water bioremediation and aquatic spawning. But these days, the Qeshm mangrove habitat has become a victim of war, and its shores are covered in tar! We might think this tragedy ends with a few blackened trees! Not at all... that's not the story at all. What has happened is much bigger than a few trees burning and turning black. A mangrove is not just a tree. It's an ecosystem; a living network on which a large part of coastal and marine life depends. When oil enters this ecosystem, it doesn't just stick to the tree trunk; it enters a complete chain of life. And perhaps to understand the scale of this disaster, no explanation is as sufficient as seeing these images. This is not just oil pollution; part of an ecosystem is being buried under oil.
