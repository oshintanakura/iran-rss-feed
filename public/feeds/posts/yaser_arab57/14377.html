Failure of the National Suicide Prevention Program

Hamid Peyravi, secretary of the Suicide Prevention Taskforce of Tehran Province, acknowledging the rising suicide rates in Iran, noted: One reason for the increase in statistics since the 1380s could be the systematization of case recording. In other words, before the 80s, cases were not recorded properly, and once accurate and precise recording was put on the agenda, naturally the statistics go up.

He referred to the issuance of the National Suicide Prevention Program in Shahrivar of 1401 and said: The plan was that in the final year of the program — that is, this year — we would achieve a 10 percent reduction in suicide attempts and deaths caused by it, but unfortunately we did not reach the program's goals at all; there has even been an increase, especially in attempts.

The vice president of the Scientific Society for Suicide Prevention in Iran called suicide bereavement one of the worst and most complicated types of grief and said: Individuals blame themselves, feel guilt, engage in self-rumination, or are subjected to society's judgment and are rejected.

He continued: More than 7,000 deaths and more than 150,000 suicide attempts in a single year have affected a large number of Iranian families, but has anyone reached out to these families? How do they cope with this suffering, and do they seek help?

Peyravi said: Each suicide involves and affects 135 people, and therefore the scope of our work goes beyond the numbers that are announced.
