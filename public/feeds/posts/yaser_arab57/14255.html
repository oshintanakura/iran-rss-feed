.✍ Toto-chan
The book "The Dream School" is the true story of Toto-chan, a little Japanese girl, during her elementary school years. A little girl who later became a global figure. Why did she become a global figure? Because her mother didn't tell her until twenty years later that she had been expelled from school in that very first grade; she only said she wanted to take her to another school that she would love more. And that is the miraculous skill that many of us parents lack. This very "not saying" of one sentence completely changed Toto-chan's future, and it is because of not saying this one sentence that today the book Toto-chan holds the title of the best-selling book in Japan's history and has been translated into dozens of languages worldwide.
I won't tell you more than this, so you can read and hear it yourself. I have read this book once and listened to its audio version twice, and while reading this book, I cried several times for the injustice that goes on in the name of upbringing, at home and at school, against the children of my homeland. So definitely read or listen to it, even if you don't have children; and send it to anyone who has children or works with children: to fathers and mothers, to trainers, to teachers, and to anyone active in the field of childhood, education, and upbringing. Today (November 10th) has been designated by the United Nations as "World Science Day for Peace and Development." I am introducing this book today to remind that science by itself does not lead to peace and development. Science only serves peace and development when it is placed in the hands of humans who are enriched within and overflowing from without. And this enrichment and overflowing happen at home and at school. Toto-chan's story is the story of how a child is enriched internally and overflows externally. Toto-chan, the child of our story, is now ninety years old and is a UNICEF Goodwill Ambassador and an advisor to the World Wide Fund for Nature.
