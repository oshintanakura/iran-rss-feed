At 61! Today I turned 61; of course, according to what's written in my ID, otherwise, according to my sisters, I was born in "Kol Kar," meaning the cotton planting season, which falls in April, from late Farvardin to mid Ordibehesht. Honestly, when I look back, I'm quite astonished at my own situation. The factors that came together to bring me from "Qaleh-ye Zardu," where I fell onto the mud floor of a dark, smoky room, to this vague present point—it's been like walking on quicksand. Was everything that happened mere coincidence? One could call it coincidence, especially in this century where not just an individual's life path but the entire universe is considered random, the result of blind forces colliding! Nevertheless, I believe every person assumes some kind of plan or destiny in the course of their life's evolution. Even those who believe in the randomness of existence are tempted, when facing grave dangers or remarkable successes, to seek an interpretation beyond pure chance in their lives. In any case, I have personally experienced something in my life with all my being: whenever, for whatever reason, my head bows from the sky toward the earth, an unpredictable and entirely unplannable event lifts my head upward. I have such faith in this experience that even if, hypothetically, all sciences and philosophies were to conclude that there's nothing up there, it would have the slightest effect on my faith in this experience! Based on my inner and outer experiences, I have accepted that not only is there something up there, but everything ends and culminates there. Hence, the most delightful and sweetest moments of my life have always been when I hold my head high. It's clear that by "up" I don't mean the geographical sense of the word. Up exists in all dimensions of being and in all directions. This too is a way of living in an era that some have perhaps wrongly or exaggeratedly called the age of the eclipse of God!#Ahmad_Zeydabadi @ahmadzeidabad
