It needs no one's permission, but... Mohammad Bagher Ghalibaf, the parliament speaker, has said: "The strategic relations between China and Iran need no one's permission." Yes, they need no one's permission, but do the Chinese really consider their relationship with Iran strategic? Mr. Ghalibaf has intended to travel to Beijing since his appointment as the Islamic Republic's special envoy for China affairs, but so far this trip has not materialized. Is this a coincidence? Or are the Chinese upset and angry over Iran's lack of negotiation with the US, the closure of the Strait of Hormuz, threats to the energy resources of Arab Gulf states by some circles in Tehran, and especially the Houthis' actions against Saudi Arabia? China, which imports more than 40 percent of its consumed oil through the Strait of Hormuz, how could it possibly be pleased with the closure of the strait? The Saudi newspaper Arab News has reported on a high-level meeting between Chinese officials and members of the Gulf Cooperation Council in Riyadh in recent days. According to the report, "Senior officials praised the remarkable progress in implementing agreements between the parties since the announcement of the strategic partnership between the GCC and China in December 2022 and discussed initiatives to strengthen this cooperation."
