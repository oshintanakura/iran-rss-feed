If Taleghani Had Read Hobbes!
The late Seyyed Mahmoud Taleghani, in the seven months of his life after the 1979 Revolution, enjoyed a popularity beyond imagination. From his words, the general public sensed a suffering, a sincerity, and a purity in which no selfish personal, factional, or ideological motive had any part. Even those segments of the people who were disgruntled with the revolution and loyal to the monarchical system sincerely held him dear and regarded him as being of a different kind. In truth, the late Taleghani was indeed of a different kind. The resonance of his clear voice in the environment of the University of Tehran acted like the blowing of a pleasant and gentle breeze upon hearts fearful and anxious about the fire of the power-seeking conflicts of the age. His voice was full of a spiritual freshness yet accompanied by much sorrow, warning, and deep concern about the future. It was as if he knew what lay ahead. He knew well many of his peers and the various political organizations of that day, and he knew precisely what goal each of them was pursuing behind their pompous and hollow slogans. He did not enter into the game of any of them, nor did he consent to the elimination and exclusion of any of them. He tried to offer a rule for the coexistence of all of them within a pluralist framework, but it was as if he knew how the story would unfold! He concealed his suffering, sorrow, distress, dissatisfaction, and desperation, but it seemed that the poisonous draught of self-consumption and self-restraint wore down his frail and ailing body and took his life. It was as if, in despair of the notables, he sought hope in the masses of the people. He held an optimism and a pleasant feeling toward the common, nameless, and unheralded people. He regarded them as beings endowed with an innate nature (fitrat), and in his view human nature was fashioned from goodness and virtue. Perhaps for this reason, he considered the delegation of the decision-making system to the masses of the people in the form of various councils to be the only path to political salvation. In his view, consultation, deliberation, and the council were the foundation of prophetic politics. For this reason, he insisted firmly on the establishment of councils at all levels of political and social life. In considering the masses of the people as endowed with innate nature, perhaps his own inclinations were also involved. Or perhaps, in his definition of human nature, he had allowed one side of the human species' disposition to dominate the other side more than it should. Whatever it was, it seemed far too idealistic for Iranian society. Perhaps Mr. Taleghani needed to read Plato through the eyes of Strauss, or Hobbes's Leviathan.
In any case, he was a human being of unparalleled integrity and, in every respect, immaculate, pure, and great—one of those of whom there should be more in the human world, but there are not. May his lofty and long-suffering soul be in the presence of God's grace and mercy at the highest degree of existence. #احمد_زیدآبادی @ahmadzeidabad
