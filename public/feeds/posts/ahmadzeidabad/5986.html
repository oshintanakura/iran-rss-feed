The logical point

Masoud Pezeshkian has presumably prepared the text of his speech for addressing the UN General Assembly, and it is unlikely that he would change it for any reason.

If I were in his place, however, I would try to occupy the "logical" position so that no justification remains for exerting pressure on the country.

The perennial issue confronting the Islamic Republic has been whether it is, in and of itself, a threat to some countries, or whether some countries are a threat to it.

If the president believes in the latter, he must state it explicitly and, for example, say: the Islamic Republic, in and of itself, is not hostile to any country in this world, and absent threats from them, all the problems that have arisen can be resolved within the framework of international laws, regulations, and norms. A clear, unambiguous, and unqualified declaration of this single point is occupying the logical position, and it will be met with a welcome from the general body of countries.

Uttering vague, general, and ambiguous sentences with the aim of satisfying all the disparate domestic forces will yield nothing but the hardship of a long journey.
