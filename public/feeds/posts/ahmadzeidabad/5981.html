A benevolent and cautious authority. I have heard valuable insights from Gholamhossein Karbaschi about the political and jurisprudential approach and personal character of Ayatollah Seyyed Mousa Shobeiri Zanjani. In the late 1340s and early 1350s, Mr. Karbaschi was one of the distinguished students of the late Mr. Shobeiri. According to what I have heard, Ayatollah Shobeiri was cautious in both jurisprudence and politics — the very principle that traditional Shia scholars regarded as a sign of piety. Caution means examining a matter from all sides before expressing an opinion or taking action — in other words, never wading into water without first finding a crossing — which is precisely what some clerics claiming revolutionary fervor have completely abandoned and cast aside! A cautious authority is extremely watchful of his tongue and his actions, is not one for controversy, does not level accusations against others, and for this reason is usually silent or very taciturn. The late Mr. Shobeiri, in addition to this, was inclined toward tolerance and benevolence in political matters. Although he did not speak of it, in practice he would act to the best of his ability to redress injustice or to free from political entanglement those he knew closely. In 1397, the presence of the late Ayatollah Shobeiri at the home of Sheikh Abdullah Nouri, along with the presence of Seyyed Mohammad Khatami, provoked the anger of Sheikh Mohammad Yazdi in his capacity as "Secretary of the Supreme Council of the Seminary"!
