The erosion of war and the capacity to think... Once again, American military drones, missiles, and bombs have rained down on the people of the country's southern cities. Alongside material damage, a number of our compatriots have been killed. A wedding ceremony in Kohestak turned into a mourning gathering. In response, Iranian forces also targeted American bases in the Middle East with missiles. Everyone is asking: how long will these clashes continue, and where will they end? The truth is that there is no clear answer to these two questions. The American side holds contradictory positions. On one hand, they emphasize continuing pressure until the collapse of our country's security and economy, and on the other hand, they claim the goal of the pressure is to force the leaders of the Islamic Republic back to the negotiating table! On the Iranian side, despite the constant insistence by responsible officials on the need for social unity and cohesion, no single voice is heard regarding the war. While the leaders of the branches of government declare their goal to be the revival of the agreement and forcing America to implement its provisions, many other voices from within the establishment insist on opposing the agreement, continuing and expanding the war, and the necessity of resorting to preemptive operations. As such, the atmosphere on both sides of the war is ambiguous and unclear. As long as this remains the case, the war will continue intermittently along its current erosive path. The main issue, however, is: to what point can the war of attrition in its current form continue? It is clear that capacities on both sides of the war are limited, and it is impossible to sustain the current conditions even for the next few months. Therefore, it is highly likely that a dramatic event will change the current course. What will be the nature of that event, and in what direction will it shape the fate of the conflict? The nature of an "event" is its chance and accidental occurrence, and it is not predictable. Any prediction in this regard is like shooting an arrow in the dark. Even if such an arrow hits its target, it is by chance and not a sign of the predictor's knowledge and skill! In our culture, these events are called fate and destiny (qaza va qadar), and now our fate is tied to fate and destiny. All we can do is pray that the outcome is good. #احمد_زیدآبادی @ahmadzeidabad
