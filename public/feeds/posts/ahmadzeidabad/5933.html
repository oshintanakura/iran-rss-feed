Order to silence! The last time I told someone it would be better to stay silent instead of speaking was in the middle of Mahmoud Ahmadinejad's first presidential term, addressing the late Seyed Ebrahim Nabavi. Of course, I didn't want him to be silent, but rather advised him to focus on satire and not get into serious political issues! Then I thought to myself, where does all this sense of wisdom, or really, my arrogance, come from? Why should I have the right to comment on political issues but Nabavi shouldn't? If he had turned around and told me: Why are you meddling in satirists' affairs for no reason? Sit down and write your serious stuff, how would I have felt? We have the right to criticize and reject others' opinions and ask them to be more careful, studied, and considerate in their expression, but where does the order to silence those whose views we don't like or don't find sound, for whatever reason, come from? I actually wrote this memory and its follow-up in support of the right to free speech of Mohammad Jafar Qaempanah, the vice president. Mr. Qaempanah has expressed an opinion about the importance and value of uranium enrichment in the country that some don't like, but instead of logical criticism, they call on him to be silent! Many also consider these people's own views to be wrong—would they like it if they were ordered to be silent by them? #احمد_زیدآبادی @ahmadzeidabad
