Order to silence! The last time I told someone they'd better stay quiet instead of talking was in the middle of Mahmoud Ahmadinejad's first term as president, addressing the late Seyed Ebrahim Nabavi. Of course, I didn't want him to be silent, but rather advised him to focus on satire and not get into serious political issues! Then I thought to myself, where does all this sense of knowingness, and indeed my own arrogance, come from? Why should I have the right to comment on political issues but Nabavi shouldn't? If he had turned around and said to me, "Why are you needlessly meddling in the affairs of satirists? Sit down and write your own serious stuff, however stale it is," how would I have felt? We have the right to critique and reject others' opinions and ask them to be more careful, studied, and considerate in their expression, but where does this order to silence and quiet those whose views we don't like or don't find sound, for whatever reason, come from? I actually wrote this memory and its subsequent reflection to support the right of Mohammad Jafar Qaempanah, the vice president, to express his opinion. Mr. Qaempanah has raised a view about the importance and value of uranium enrichment in the country, which some have not liked, but instead of logical criticism, they are calling for his silence and quiet! Many also consider the views of these very people to be wrong—would they be pleased if they were ordered to be silent by them? #Ahmad_Zeidabadi @ahmadzeidabad
