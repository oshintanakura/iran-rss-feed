The future of petrochemicals runs through the heart of energy imbalance: "More value with less energy." Hesam Khoshbinfar, CEO of Amir Kabir Petrochemical Company, in a note published in the "Jame Jam" newspaper, pointing to constraints in the supply of gas, electricity, feedstock, and ancillary services, emphasized that Iran's petrochemical industry stands on the verge of a shift in approach — a shift from "maximizing production" toward "maximizing value per unit of energy and feedstock." According to him, under the new conditions, the number of tons of product produced is no longer a sufficient measure of performance on its own; one must ask how much economic value is generated for the country from each unit of energy and feedstock. Khoshbinfar also deemed the completion of the value chain essential and stressed moving from "selling product" toward "selling value" through the development of downstream industries, higher value-added products, and linking large complexes with knowledge-based companies and investors. The CEO of Amir Kabir Petrochemical went on to describe resilience as part of the concept of production, listing the sustainability of feedstock and energy, human resource readiness, supply of critical parts, equipment reliability, and the ability to quickly return to the production cycle as requirements of the industry of the future.
