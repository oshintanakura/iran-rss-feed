🔸 The twenty-first of September each year, which in the Solar Hijri calendar usually falls on the 30th of Shahrivar, has been designated the International Day of Peace.
🔸 By proclaiming this day, the United Nations calls on nations and governments to observe a full day of ceasefire and to promote a culture of peace. The peace bell is rung at the organization's headquarters in New York, and messages about peaceful coexistence are broadcast. Yet on the very same days when these slogans are repeated, Western fighter jets soar over various parts of the world, and weapons shipments pour from the ports of countries that claim to champion peace into conflict zones. The contradiction between rhetoric and action lays bare, more than anything else, the dual nature of the Western order.
🔸 Ultimately, the impact of this double standard on future generations in war-torn regions is a subject rarely touched upon in the official ceremonies of the Day of Peace. Children who grow up under the shadow of bombardment, or communities worn down by sanctions and foreign intervention, benefit least from global slogans.
