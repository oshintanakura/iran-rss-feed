📚 Journalism of War and Peace | Author: Abdolreza Aalami | First Edition | Naqd-e Farhang Publications | 2026 | In a world where every armed conflict is narrated on news pages and in cyberspace before it is decided on the battlefield, this is the fundamental question: What responsibility does a journalist have? This book examines two distinct approaches to media coverage of conflicts: war journalism, which focuses on violence, dramatization, and winner-loser narratives, and peace journalism, which places the roots of conflict, hidden voices, and ways out at the center of attention. These two approaches not only define the method of reporting but also influence public opinion, political decisions, and even the course of conflicts. Drawing on the theories of Johan Galtung, the founder of the concept of peace journalism, and the views of Jake Lynch and Annabel McGoldrick, this book blends theory with field experience. Case studies from Gaza, Ukraine, Syria, and Sudan show how editorial choices, from camera angle to source selection, can make war seem natural or peace possible. The book also addresses the new challenges of journalism in the digital age: cyber warfare, misinformation, and cyberspace as a parallel battlefield whose rules are still being formed. This book is written for journalism and communications students, field journalists, and any curious reader who wants to understand how the media narrate war—and what that narration costs.
