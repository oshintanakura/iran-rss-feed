Civilizational Iranian-Islamic Intellectualism ✍️ Seyyed Mohammad Javad Bani Saeed Langeroudi. Intellectualism in Iran after the Islamic Revolution is not merely faced with the issue of critiquing tradition or adapting and localizing new theories; rather, the more fundamental issue is the possibility of the formation of "Iranian-Islamic civilizational intellectualism": an intellectualism that can create a coherent, rational, and path-opening system in the relationship between truth and reality, revelation and reason, identity and progress, heritage and future, and historical belonging and social responsibility. This article, with a conceptual, normative, and civilizational approach, addresses the formulation of the model of the civilizational intellectual. Its main claim is that this model is neither limited to imitating dominant global concepts and theoretical frameworks, nor is it reduced to an uncritical defense of the past and tradition, nor does it see its function as justifying the status quo; rather, it is an epistemic, moral, and social activism that, relying on revelatory sources, demonstrative rationality, the historical experience of Iran and Islam, and a precise understanding of contemporary issues and developments, seeks to produce concepts, theorize, and open the civilizational horizon of society. Civilizational intellectualism is truth-oriented and independent in knowledge; it understands religion as a system with explanatory capacity and social orientation; it reconciles critique and defense, loyalty and reform, and idealism and realism; and it regards critique not merely as negation and denial, but as a prelude to establishment, offering alternatives, and institution-building. Epistemic independence, conscious historical belonging, conceptual production, problem-orientation, foundational critique, responsible presence in the public sphere, the ability to combine rationality and spirituality, and attention to education and institution-building are among the fundamental components of this model.
