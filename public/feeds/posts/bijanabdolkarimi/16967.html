The Crisis of Self-Defeating and Short-Sighted Intellectualism ✍️ Seyyed Mohammad Javad Bani Saeed Langeroudi

Decades after the victory of Iran's Islamic Revolution, one of the fundamental questions in the realm of social and cultural thought is: what relationship have intellectual elites and so-called intellectual currents established with the reconstruction of the national and religious identity of Iranian society? The historical expectation of the Iranian Muslim intellectual was that, in the face of the extensive epistemological, media, civilizational, and lifestyle onslaught of opposing currents, they would creatively revisit the heritage of Iran, Islam, and Islamic-Iranian civilization, and provide a new apparatus for its defense, explanation, and extension. However, in a significant portion of this current, instead of such a mission, one observes a kind of theoretical passivity, infatuation with modernity and postmodernity, distrust of indigenous heritage, and even alignment with aggressive discourses. This article, with a critical approach, examines the identity crisis of dependent intellectualism, its inability to formulate a new Iranian-Islamic identity, and the cultural, legal, economic, and social consequences of this inefficiency.
