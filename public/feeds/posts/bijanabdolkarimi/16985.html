📚 Toward a Revolution in the Statism Paradigm (Rethinking Governance in Light of Global Transformations) | Volume One: Theoretical Pathology of Governance | Authors: Reza Shahran, Hossein Vazifeh-Ali | First Edition | Naqd-e Farhang Publishing | 1405 | The modern state has been one of the most successful institutions in human history. Security, public education, economic development, the formation of modern nations, and many achievements of the contemporary world would have been inconceivable without it. But can a structure formed to solve the problems of centuries past still respond to today's networked world, artificial intelligence, the digital economy, and interconnected societies? This book begins with a fundamental question: Why do many governance systems, despite repeated reforms, changes of government, restructuring, and accumulated experience, still find themselves reproducing similar crises, imbalances, and dissatisfactions? Drawing on political philosophy, governance theory, complexity science, futures studies, and historical experience, the author shows that many contemporary problems cannot be understood merely at the level of performance, institutions, or policies; instead, we must return to the deeper layer of governance theories and the foundational assumptions upon which these structures were built. Toward a Revolution in the Statism Paradigm is, before it is a book about solutions, a book about diagnosing the problem — an effort to examine the pathology of dominant governance theories and to identify the source of the blockages that manifest as inefficiency, concentration of power, legitimacy crises, and the inability to solve complex problems. This work is the first volume of a broader research project. In subsequent volumes, building on the pathologies presented in this book, an alternative theory of governance will be developed — one that seeks to articulate, within a coherent framework, the capacities of the networked world, human agency, and the distribution of knowledge and power. A transition model suited to Iran's conditions will also be presented, spanning from the level of theoretical foundations to that of macro-policymaking and institutional design, in order to explore the possibility of a gradual movement from the existing situation toward emerging orders.
