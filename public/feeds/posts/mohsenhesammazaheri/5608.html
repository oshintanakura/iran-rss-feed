With the widespread rise of Islamic movements over the past three or four decades, what is called "Islamic jurisprudence" or "Sharia" has increasingly assumed a central place in the language of politics and its practices—particularly among Islamists, and in the Western world as well. Popular narratives and a great many false claims have distorted Sharia to such an extent that distinguishing between its principles and practices in the past and its modern, politicized incarnations has become difficult. This book sets out to bring these differences to light: to present Sharia as a belief and practice in the past; to examine how it operated in society and within the framework of the virtuous ummah; and to show how Sharia interacted with the state and how it has in fact become a modern instrument wielded especially by nation-states.
