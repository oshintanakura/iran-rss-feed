🔸The Loss of a Mediator🔸

If we were to describe any figure with a single word, the word that fits Ayatollah Seyyed Mousa Shubeyri Zanjani (who passed away last night) is "mediator."

Though he was considered among the traditionalist jurists of the seminary in his jurisprudential leanings, and though—as is clear from his scattered remarks and writings (including in the highly readable book collection "A Sip from the Sea")—he was not without taste or conviction in politics and history, he nonetheless had his own distinct and particular social and political bearing. Over the past two or three decades, he played a role that others were incapable of playing: that of "mediator" between the system and the religious currents and individuals it had cast out. Shubeyri Zanjani was a "dragoman" for translating between two groups that did not understand each other's language. For this reason, knots that could not be untied through law and conventional political methods were sometimes loosened by a single phone call from him, and challenges that could have spiraled into crises were sometimes defused by a single piece of advice from him. These mediations were, of course, typically behind the scenes and kept out of the media—and that, too, was part of his way.

Because of this role he played, Shubeyri was beloved by all sides. He kept company with the martyred Leader and also performed funeral prayers for clerics critical of the system, and the door of his home remained open every day to all political and religious currents. Undoubtedly, his humble and tolerant character also played a part in this.

In religious matters as well, although he is classified among the traditional jurists, he took a different approach there too. Unlike them, he did not use his position, standing, and influence to pursue religious identity-driven concerns, nor did he seek to pile up a calendar of Shiite commemorations and to fatten rituals. On the contrary, he was critical of the excessive expansion of rituals.

That said, if the loss of Mr. Shubeyri Zanjani is a scholarly loss for the seminaries, it will be a functional and institutional blow to the system and to Iranian society as well—one that could disrupt the system's relations with certain seminary and religious currents. Though Shubeyri was not counted among the first-tier maraji in terms of the organizational hierarchy of the marja'iyya, had no vast apparatus or numerous offices, and his following did not extend beyond a circle of certain seminary insiders and his fellow townspeople, his loss creates a void in the institution of Shiite marja'iyya in Iran that cannot be filled—especially in the current critical conditions, when Iranian society needs more than ever those who can play the role of mediator in various arenas.
