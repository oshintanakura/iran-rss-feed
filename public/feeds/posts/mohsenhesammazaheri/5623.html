🔸The Loss of a Mediator🔸

If we were to describe any figure with a single word, the word that fits Ayatollah Seyyed Mousa Shubeyri Zanjani (who passed away last night) is "mediator."

Despite being considered, in terms of his jurisprudential orientation, among the traditionalist jurists of the seminary, and despite not being devoid of taste or conviction in politics and history—as is evident from his scattered remarks and writings (including in the highly readable book collection "A Sip from the Sea")—he had his own distinct and particular social and political demeanor. In the past two or three decades, he played a role that others were incapable of fulfilling: the role of "mediator" between the system and the religious currents and individuals it had cast out. Shubeyri Zanjani was a "dragoman" for translating the words of two groups who did not understand each other's language. That is why knots that could not be untied through law and conventional political methods were sometimes loosened by a single phone call from him, and challenges that could have spiraled into crises were sometimes defused by a single piece of advice from him. These mediations were, of course, typically behind the scenes and never publicized—and that, too, was part of his way of being.

Because of this role he played, Shubeyri was beloved by all sides. He both associated with the martyred Leader and would recite the funeral prayer for clerics critical of the system, and the door of his home remained open every day to all political and religious currents. Undoubtedly, his humble and tolerant character also contributed to this.

In religious matters as well, although he is classified among the traditional jurists, he took a different approach there too. Unlike them, he did not use his position, standing, and influence to pursue religious identity-driven concerns, and did not seek to pile up a calendar of Shia occasions and to fatten rituals. On the contrary, he was critical of the excessive expansion of rituals.

That said, if the loss of Mr. Shubeyri Zanjani is considered a scholarly loss for the seminaries, it will be a functional and institutional blow for the system and for Iranian society as well—one that could disrupt the relations between the system and certain seminary and religious currents. Although Shubeyri was not considered among the first-tier maraji' in terms of the organizational structure of the marja'iyya, and did not have extensive organizations and numerous offices, and the number of his followers did not extend beyond the circle of certain seminary elites and his fellow townspeople, his loss creates a void in the institution of Shia marja'iyya in Iran that cannot be filled—especially in the current critical conditions, when Iranian society, more than ever, needs those who play the role of mediator in various arenas.
