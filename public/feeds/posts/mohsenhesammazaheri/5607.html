🔸A peace that never made it to the calendar🔸 ✍ Mohsen Hesam Mazaheri [Part 2 and final]
4The formation of calendars and their changes are not without reason. No occasion is remembered by chance or turned into a memory; just as no occasion is forgotten by accident. Which occasions and which figures are summoned from the depths of history and which are consigned to the archives depends on the needs and exigencies of society. More than having a historical root, it carries sociological significance and is influenced by the requirements, experiences, and necessities of society, as well as being the outcome of the will of power institutions at any given moment. If an event or a figure is recorded in the calendar, it is because it has a bearing on today and is meant to respond to a need in society or in power. And likewise, if it is not there, it means its presence was not necessary. This is why examining the course of changes in societies' calendars (both official and unofficial) allows us to understand them and the path of their transformations.
5With that in mind, the very fact that today's conventional Shia, for example, knows the day of Lady Masoumeh's arrival in Qom and honors it, or holds mourning ceremonies for the passing of Lady Ruqayya, yet does not recognize the day of peace of their second Imam, can explain part of our present.
6Who knows. Perhaps one day the peace of Imam Hasan will be added to the Shia calendar, just as in our time there are not few occasions that have been added to ritual calendars without the Shia of earlier eras having honored them. The calendar is a living entity, just like society.
