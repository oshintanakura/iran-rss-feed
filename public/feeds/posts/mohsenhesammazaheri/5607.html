🔸A peace that never made it to the calendar🔸 ✍ Mohsen Hesam Mazaheri [Part 2 and Final] 4 The formation of calendars and their changes are not without reason. No occasion is remembered by chance and turned into a memory; just as no occasion is forgotten by accident. Which occasions and which figures are summoned from the depths of history and which are relegated to the archives depends on the needs and exigencies of society. More than having a historical root, it carries sociological significance and is influenced by the requirements, experiences, and necessities of society, as well as being the outcome of the will of power institutions at any given moment. If an event or a figure is recorded in the calendar, it is because it has a bearing on today and is meant to address a need in society or in power. And likewise, if it is not, it means its presence was not necessary. It is for this reason that by examining the course of changes in societies' calendars (both official and unofficial), one can understand them and the path of their transformations. 5 With this in mind, the very fact that today's conventional Shia, for example, knows the day of Lady Masoumeh's arrival in Qom and honors it, or holds mourning ceremonies for the passing of Lady Ruqayyah, but does not recognize the peace day of their second Imam, can explain a part of our present. 6 Of course, who knows.
