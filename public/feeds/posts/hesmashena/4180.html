This separation prevents the organization from getting bogged down in implementation details and losing its main mission, which is increasing the overall system's productivity. The ultimate success criterion: The program's success should not be measured by the number of heaters, coolers, buses, or motorcycles replaced. The right measure is the amount of service produced with fewer resources. In cooling: reduced electricity, water, and peak demand against maintained comfort. In heating: reduced gas and winter peak against maintained comfort. In mobility: energy consumed per passenger-kilometer or ton-kilometer. At the macro level, the final indicator is: providing more or better cooling, heating, and mobility with less water consumption, less damage to air and soil, less energy, and lower public cost. This framework can transform the organization's 24-month program from a set of scattered projects into a coherent and measurable policy. In this view, gasoline, gas, electricity, coolers, heaters, boiler rooms, and vehicles are not themselves the "problem"; they are tools for providing three main services with higher efficiency and less pressure on the country's four basic resources.
