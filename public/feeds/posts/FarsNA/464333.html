False cheating accusations against students due to software errors

🔹AI detectors used to identify cheating among school and university students have come under scrutiny in new research for significant errors, in some cases mistakenly flagging completely human-written text as AI-generated.

🔹Investigations show these tools do not detect the true origin of the text; instead, they calculate the probability of AI-generated text based on statistical features and writing style.

🔹In one experiment, a completely human-written text was assessed by 5 commercial tools as anywhere from "zero percent" to "100 percent" AI-generated.

🔹These systems have also been reported to make more errors with people whose first language is not English; in a study of 91 real TOEFL essays, an average of 61 percent of human-written texts were mistakenly identified as AI-generated.

🔹Following these problems, universities such as Waterloo in Canada and Cape Town have stopped using Turnitin's AI detection score and have emphasized relying on evidence such as drafts, edit history, learning process, and conversations with the student.
