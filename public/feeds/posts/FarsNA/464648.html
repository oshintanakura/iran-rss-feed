The case of the online gold dealer has reached the Supreme National Security Council

🔹The online gold sales platform Milli Gold, in a letter to Mohsen Rezaei, Secretary of the Supreme National Security Council, has called for an urgent order to lift restrictions on users' access to their gold held in bank vaults.

🔹The platform says restrictions imposed by the Economic Security Police and some related bodies have made it difficult to access part of its reserves and fulfill its obligations to users.

🔹In recent days, a number of Milli Gold users on social media have complained about delays in rial settlements and in receiving their physical gold. Some users have also raised concerns about the actual amount of gold backing the platform's transactions, alleging "selling empty."

🔹These concerns come as, under Central Bank regulations, online platforms must hold reserves equivalent to their obligations for gold sold, and the stored gold must be converted into standard bars with a purity of at least 995 within three months.

🔹Milli Gold, however, claims that existing governance mechanisms and bureaucracy have restricted its access to bank reserves, and that this has in turn disrupted the delivery of users' gold. The company had previously stated that users' gold is held in bank vaults.

🔸Now, given recent concerns about users' access to their gold and the claims raised about the backing of transactions, connecting all platforms to the supervisory system as quickly as possible — with online reporting of their inventory and obligations — has become more important than ever and could ease some of users' concerns.
