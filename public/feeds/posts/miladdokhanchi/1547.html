Let us revive leadership!

Within each of us dwells a sultan and a shepherd. The sultan rules and controls; the inner shepherd guides. The sultan leans toward subjugation, domination, and the management of affairs, while the shepherd leans toward guidance, opening new horizons, and stepping into the unknown.

In the language of business, the inner sultan manages and the shepherd leads. The sultan is necessary for managing affairs; the shepherd, for creation, bringing forth, and breaking down the walls of human limitation. Sultanhood gravitates toward the individual; shepherdliness, toward the collective.

In the duality of sultan and shepherd, the question is not one of preference but of balance — and balance becomes possible when we have not only erected the apparatuses of our inner sultanate but also passed beyond them and brought inner leadership into emergence. Yes, both the sultan and the shepherd are needed, but if we are to envision an ultimate destination, that destination is the passage from the inner sultanate to the inner shepherdliness.

Shepherdliness and leadership, then, are not a position. Leadership is a particular way of thinking and being; leadership is a path — in the first instance a personal path that then becomes determinate outwardly and takes on a collective form. The question: shepherd or sultan?

In the process leading to the Islamic Revolution, Iranian society managed to pass beyond sultanate and activate its inner shepherd — and it was this discovered inner shepherd that emerged outwardly, and from its heart, the concept of "Imam" was born. In Iran, leadership emerged because every human being saw themselves as a leader; because leadership emerged as a way of being; because an inner revolution took place in our inner states, and this caused leadership as a collective matter to also appear in Article 43 of the Constitution: "The organization of the country's economic program... such that every individual... has sufficient ability to participate actively in the leadership of the country..."

But in post-revolutionary Iran, leadership was reduced to a position. Once again our inner sultanic relations were activated, and we were degraded from leaders who discovered "leaders" to followers who merely follow. From then on, following, imitation, and the rejection of responsibility became our common behavioral currency. Everywhere: politics, art, culture, economy...

To solve Iran's problems, each of us must first and once again become a leader. We must pass beyond our inner sultanates and activate our inner shepherds; and it is these inner shepherds that, when actualized, make shepherdliness possible outwardly. Without leadership as a plural matter, political and social leadership is also impossible; what emerges without it is not leadership — it is the same sultan wearing the shepherd's clothing.

Let us activate our inner shepherd.

Let us all be leaders.
