Few books in Iran's contemporary history have created as many supporters and opponents as Jalal Al-e-Ahmad's "Weststruckness." Some consider it a manifesto of return to self. Others see it as the beginning of a great misunderstanding. Usually, such texts have been read far less than the talk and rumors surrounding them; perhaps before judging this book, we need to read it once carefully, historically, and critically. In the re-reading course of "Weststruckness," we are not just going to read the famous text of Jalal Al-e-Ahmad from beginning to end; we are going to place this book within the context of the history of contemporary Iranian thought.
