Joint commitment on accountability for frontier models

To build a hopeful future for the people of America and around the world, we believe that every company has a duty to develop its technology safely and in a way that builds trust with customers and the general public.

The starting point on this path is that all companies engaged in training and deploying frontier models must have strong internal control processes and mechanisms in place to ensure that their technology performs in line with defined objectives and that any potential flaw is immediately identified and addressed. Therefore, in addition to the usual precautionary measures, we believe that every company must implement the following four layers of oversight and auditing:

1. Implementation of rigorous internal control mechanisms to monitor the capabilities and alignment of models during the training and deployment stages, particularly in areas such as cybersecurity, biosecurity, and chemical threats, and to ensure that systems do not engage in intrusion or unauthorized and unintended access to technical infrastructure.

2. Delegation of authority to an internal team to ensure that all control, oversight, and detection systems operate in accordance with established standards and that identified disruptions are resolved without delay.

3. Cooperation with independent external evaluators and auditors to conduct impartial inspections of the correctness of oversight, monitoring, and error detection processes.

4. Appointment of an independent committee drawn from board members for high-level oversight, receiving periodic reports from executive teams and internal and external auditors, and ensuring the complete resolution of all reported deficiencies.

Together, these steps will provide lasting reassurance to companies, customers, and society that the technology in question performs reliably and in line with its original objectives.

Companies that are members of this agreement will meet regularly to develop standards and best practices for enhancing the safety of their systems.

Over time, codifying these mechanisms within the framework of formal laws or regulations will be a logical approach. Regardless of whether the implementation of these rules acquires a legally binding character for enterprises in the future, we consider the implementation of these controls and audits a vital necessity for guaranteeing a safe future for all, and each and every one of our organizations is committed to realizing it.
