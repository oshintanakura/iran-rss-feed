📝 The Fifty-Fifth Anniversary of the 2500-Year Celebrations; The Eyes of the World on the Glory of Ancient Iran

On the twentieth of Mehr, 1350 Solar Hijri, equivalent to 2530 Imperial, around 11 AM, the helicopter carrying Mohammad Reza Shah Pahlavi and Empress Farah touched down at Pasargadae. The Shah of Iran stood before the tomb of Cyrus the Great, founder of the Achaemenid Empire, and laid a bouquet of flowers upon his tomb as a gesture of respect and homage. The Achaemenid flag and the Imperial flag of Iran were raised, and with a 101-gun salute, the official ceremony commemorating the founding of the Iranian Empire began — a ceremony meant to showcase the glory of one of the world's oldest civilizations before the eyes of the world once again after 25 centuries. Now, 55 years have passed since those days.

At the opening ceremony of the 2500-year celebrations, members of the royal family, the Prime Minister, the heads of the Senate and the National Consultative Assembly, high-ranking civil and military officials, Iranologists, and representatives of various religions were present. Mohammad Reza Shah stood before the tomb of Cyrus and began his historic speech by paying tribute to the founder of the Achaemenid Empire: "Cyrus! Great King, King of Kings, Achaemenid King, King of the land of Iran, we have gathered today before your eternal tomb to tell you: rest easy, for we are awake and we shall always remain awake to guard your proud heritage."
