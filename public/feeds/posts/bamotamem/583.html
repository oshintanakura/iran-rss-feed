Strategy hurts (1)

Following up on my earlier point about the vision, it's worth adding a few more notes. I've said these things many times over the years. But repeating them is useful too. They're worth constantly repeating and hearing.

My friends.

Strategy is always and invariably accompanied by resource allocation. The resources at our disposal (money, time, connections, etc.) are limited, and strategy is supposed to help us allocate these limited resources more effectively.

"Resource allocation" is different from "resource distribution."

Resource distribution means I spread whatever I have across different tasks, different resources, different departments, and try to make sure everyone gets a piece of the pie.

Resource allocation is a precise scientific term with a specific meaning.

Resource allocation means we think about who we want to give these limited resources to (and who we don't).

By defining and executing strategy, we always create dissatisfied people. If you see that a strategy meeting was held, everyone sat around and talked, and at the end everyone came out smiling, know that the only thing wasted there was the sugar, tea, and fruit of the meeting.

Must, must, must, some people come out of the strategy meeting upset. These are the same managers, the same departments, and the same projects that have been allocated fewer resources.
