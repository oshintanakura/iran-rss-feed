Scoring in negotiation (from writings eighteen years ago) One of the rules of giving concessions in a negotiation is that if you give the other party a concession that you thought was valuable and appropriate, and they didn't accept it, or they agreed and then backed out, don't easily give that same concession again later (either give them less, or take more from them, and don't easily go back to the previous agreement). Otherwise, they might not appreciate the concession they receive again. Shopkeepers know this too. For example, when they say they'll give 20% off on something, they give this discount on the condition that the person accepts and buys it right there in the store. But if they say, 'Let me go take a walk and come back,' when they return, either they're told it's sold out, or they say we checked the system and the price is higher, or they say we called the store owner and he said that discount wasn't for this item. It's the same when landing projects. If you tell a client you can do something (at no extra cost) two weeks earlier than the norm, and you see they don't appreciate it and easily pass it by, take a look at your calendar and say, 'I made a mistake.' So they learn to appreciate it when they get a good concession. You might lose out in some places, but in the long run it has many benefits. This rule is followed at all levels of negotiation, from everyday transactions to equations and international relations, and it should be.
