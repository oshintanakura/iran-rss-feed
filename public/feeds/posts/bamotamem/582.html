"A collection of all good things" is not a strategy

Yesterday I watched the conversation between Mr. Abdeh Tabrizi and Mr. Mohammad Fazeli.

I hold both of these dear gentlemen in respect, and the points Mr. Abdeh Tabrizi made about macroeconomics were certainly instructive for me and all his students (as they always have been).

But at one point in the conversation, Mr. Abdeh Tabrizi said about the 20-year Vision Document: "We drafted this document in 1384, when Ahmadinejad had not yet come to power, and the document's goals seemed achievable."

Linking the failure to realize the Vision Document to Ahmadinejad's coming to power (which certainly dealt a great blow to national interests) does not seem logical to me.

The Vision Document was a completely wrong document from the very beginning. It was a beautifully written essay that had no strategic nature (while an upstream document of this kind should naturally have a strategic nature).

This document was "a collection of all good and attractive things." Strategy is not of this kind. Strategy means letting go of many good things and setting priorities among other good things, and specifying which good thing we are willing to sacrifice for the sake of another good thing.

If these questions are not answered in a document, that document is nothing more than an essay.

I consider it one of my honors that from those very years, when I was teaching strategy, I taught this document as an example of a completely wrong document, and I told my students that this document is not a vision—it is merely eye-catching.

That document was wrong on the first day too; with Ahmadinejad or without Ahmadinejad, for Iran or for Saudi Arabia or for America.

It was a beautiful essay that seemed poetic and attractive. But my judgment is that anyone with even a little literacy in strategy would never have been willing to sign beneath that essay.
