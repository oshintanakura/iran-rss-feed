On the facade of the main building of the US Post Office in New York (the Farley Building), a sentence is engraved that you may have seen before in photos: "Neither snow nor rain nor heat nor gloom of night stays these couriers from the swift completion of their appointed rounds." The sentence was engraved in 1914 and is so famous that many consider it the official motto of the US Postal Service. In his book "Neither Snow Nor Rain," which is dedicated to the history of the US Postal Service, David Leonard tells the story behind this sentence. The sentence was used by Herodotus in his famous book, Histories, to describe the Iranian couriers during the era of Darius the Great. The Iranian postal industry began during the Achaemenid Empire under Cyrus the Great and reached its peak during the era of Darius the Great, and was known as the fastest organized postal system in the world (the Egyptians and Chinese also had postal systems, but the symbol of fast and organized mail was Iran). Leonard ends the story of how this sentence was chosen and engraved as follows: Many think this sentence is the motto of the US Postal Service. But this sentence is merely the homage of the world's greatest postal system to one of its most renowned ancestors. #هایلایت
