In the Motamem systems thinking discussion about Jared Diamond, one of the members had quoted something from Jared Diamond, and it occurred to me to write a bit there about his latest book, which was published last month. Naturally, there's more room for writing at length on Motamem. But still, I can share a few general points here as well:🖋️ In this book (and in almost all his books from the past thirty years), Diamond is pondering this question: which is greater — the power of "systems and structure" or the power of "people"?I think we Iranians are constantly thinking about this very question. That if so-and-so hadn't been there or hadn't come in such-and-such a year, or if someone else had come instead of someone else, how much would our historical trajectory have changed?🖋️ There are plenty of fascinating points and instructive stories in this book. But methodologically and analytically, I think it's weaker than Diamond's previous works. Still, by a wide margin, Guns, Germs, and Steel remains his best book.🖋️ Diamond believes in the power of influence for both individuals and systems. But he considers the effect of individuals greater in the short term and the effect of systems greater in the long term.🖋️ Diamond is old. One should always view the latest works of thinkers and writers with a forgiving eye. Because their goal is to deliver their remaining notes to us.
