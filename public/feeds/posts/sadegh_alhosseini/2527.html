Is the dollar expensive or cheap today?
Sadegh Al-Hosseini
This chart shows the dollar exchange rate at today's prices; that is, each past rate has been adjusted for Iran's inflation so it can be compared with today's money. The result is:
• The peak of Mehr and Aban 1397: about 19 thousand tomans, equivalent to 455 thousand tomans today
• In 1399: the rate was about 25 thousand tomans, equivalent to 300 thousand tomans today
• Esfand 1401: the rate of 53 thousand tomans, equivalent to 241 thousand tomans today
So today's dollar, in real terms, is about half the peak of 1397 and roughly at the same level as Esfand 1401 and Khordad 1398. What has happened in these years is mostly the collapse of the rial's value rather than a real rise in the dollar's price. So what matters is the growth of liquidity, not even sanctions and siege!
Big nominal numbers deceive us; the only correct measure is the inflation-adjusted rate.
Important point: the real rate being low compared to historical peaks does not mean the dollar will remain cheap in the future. That is, the recent nominal jumps have only compensated for accumulated inflation, not gone beyond it.
