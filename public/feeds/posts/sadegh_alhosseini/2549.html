Will Iran launch a preemptive strike before the American election? In Washington, this scenario is being taken seriously. The logic goes like this: Tehran's clock is an hourglass, and every day the sanctions erode the economy further. If Tehran is certain that after November 2 it will be attacked regardless, waiting means losing. So the best moment to strike is a few weeks before the election. Iran would both demonstrate its power and knock Trump down at the ballot box, because any response from America would drag the war into the middle of election season. But this calculation has one big hole: January 3, the day the new American Congress begins its work. From November 2 until that day, Trump is neither worried about votes nor dealing with a new Congress. On the other side, until the election, Trump sees any war as harmful to himself and buys time. Every day he buys time is an advantage for Tehran and reduces the incentive to preempt. So everything comes back to a question that James Fearon has called the "commitment problem": Does Tehran believe that after the election it will not be attacked? If it believes so, preemption makes no sense. If it does not, Schelling's dark room may at any moment ring out with the sound of gunfire.@sadegh_alhosseini
