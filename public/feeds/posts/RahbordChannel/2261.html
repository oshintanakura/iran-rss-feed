The story of Shir Del, oil, and the Strait of Hormuz. Trump has threatened that if the Iranians do not accept his conditions—namely, a complete halt to enrichment, missile restrictions, and not supporting his proxy groups in the region—he will resort to military options. Iran's situation is also not good for various reasons, and in response to the Yankees' threat, it has said that the card of closing the Strait of Hormuz is on the table, and of course, the oil facilities of neighboring countries will not be safe either. In a report from the Center for Strategic and International Studies, four oil scenarios have been outlined, and reviewing them is worthwhile: The first scenario is America preventing Iran's production and seizing its exports; Kharg Island and oil tankers are the main targets, and the consequence is cutting 1.6 million barrels of daily exports to China; in this relatively low-risk scenario for the Yankees, we will see a $10-12 increase in crude oil prices. The second scenario is Iran disrupting shipping in the Persian Gulf, carried out with speedboats, drones, anti-ship missiles, and mining in the Strait of Hormuz; here, about 18 million barrels of Arab countries' oil exports are at risk. We will witness oil prices jumping above $90, which will also push gasoline prices in America above $3. The UAE and Saudi Arabia can use alternative routes to the Strait of Hormuz to a very limited extent, but Iraq, Kuwait, and Qatar have no way to export their oil except through the Strait of Hormuz. The third scenario is an American attack on Iran's oil facilities, aimed at destroying the loading docks of Kharg Island, oil platforms, and oil infrastructure, and oil prices will likely rise above $100 because Iranian oil will be out of the market for a long time. The fourth scenario is a direct Iranian attack on the oil facilities of neighboring Arab countries, targeting oil fields, refineries, and export terminals of Saudi Arabia, Iraq, the UAE, and other countries, and similar to the time of Russia's attack on Ukraine, it might even push oil prices above $130. As a personal opinion, scenarios two and four can create deterrence as a threat, but if implemented, they would have serious consequences on the scale of regime change for the Islamic Republic, and given the current situation, I doubt they are on Tehran's agenda right now.
